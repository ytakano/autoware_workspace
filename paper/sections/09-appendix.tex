\section{NDT Align: Pseudocode and Cost-Model Correspondence}
\label{app:pseudocode}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:ndt-align} states the shipped \ndt{} align kernel at the specification
level, so that each factor of the execution-time decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp} maps to a
concrete step. Line-search derivative refinement is disabled in the shipped configuration,
so the loop performs one initial derivative pass (line~\ref{ln:init}) plus one pass per
Newton step (line~\ref{ln:pass}), giving $N_{\mathrm{pass}}=N_{\mathrm{iter}}+1$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{$\textsc{NDT-Align}(\mathrm{map}, \mathrm{source}, \mathrm{guess})$ --- shipped
serial kernel; $\mathrm{cfg}$ holds $I_{\max}$, $\epsilon$ (\texttt{trans\_epsilon}), and the
step-size cap.}
\label{alg:ndt-align}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{DerivPass}{$\mathrm{map}, \mathrm{source}, \mathbf{x}$}
  \State $(\mathrm{score}, \mathbf{g}, \mathbf{H}) \gets (0, \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{0})$
  \For{$p \gets 1$ \textbf{to} $P$} \Comment{$P=\lvert\mathrm{source}\rvert$}
    \State $q \gets \textsc{Transform}(\mathbf{x}, \mathrm{source}[p])$
    \State $\mathcal{N} \gets \textsc{RadiusSearch}(\mathrm{map.tree}, q)$ \label{ln:search}
       \Comment{returns $K(p)\le\maxnn$ leaves}
    \For{$\ell \in \mathcal{N}$} \label{ln:kernel}
       \Comment{per point--leaf score/gradient/Hessian}
      \State $(\mathrm{score}, \mathbf{g}, \mathbf{H}) \mathrel{+}= \textsc{LeafTerm}(q, \ell)$
    \EndFor
  \EndFor
  \State \Return $(\mathrm{score}, \mathbf{g}, \mathbf{H})$
\EndFunction
\Statex
\State $\mathbf{x} \gets \textsc{EulerOf}(\mathrm{guess})$;\quad $i \gets 0$
\State $(\mathrm{score}, \mathbf{g}, \mathbf{H}) \gets \Call{DerivPass}{\mathrm{map}, \mathrm{source}, \mathbf{x}}$ \label{ln:init}
   \Comment{initial pass}
\While{$\Delta \gets \textsc{SolveNewton}(\mathbf{H}, -\mathbf{g})$ \textbf{succeeds}} \label{ln:solve}
  \State $a \gets \mathrm{clamp}(\lVert\Delta\rVert,\ \epsilon/2,\ \mathrm{cfg.step})$;\quad
         $\mathbf{x} \gets \mathbf{x} + a\,\widehat{\Delta}$ \label{ln:update}
     \Comment{step length + pose update}
  \State $(\mathrm{score}, \mathbf{g}, \mathbf{H}) \gets \Call{DerivPass}{\mathrm{map}, \mathrm{source}, \mathbf{x}}$ \label{ln:pass}
  \State $i \gets i + 1$
  \If{$i \ge I_{\max}$ \textbf{or} $a < \epsilon$} \textbf{break} \EndIf \label{ln:conv}
\EndWhile
\State \Return $(\mathbf{x}, i)$ \Comment{$i = N_{\mathrm{iter}}$}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Table~\ref{tab:eqmap} maps every symbol of~\eqref{eq:decomp} to its step. The bounds
of Sec.~\ref{sec:decomposition} follow directly: $\textsc{DerivPass}$ runs
$N_{\mathrm{pass}}=N_{\mathrm{iter}}+1$ times, its point loop $P$ times, and its leaf loop
$K(p)\le\maxnn$ times, while $\textsc{SolveNewton}$ and the pose update run once per
Newton iteration ($N_{\mathrm{iter}}\le I_{\max}$ times). The pseudocode is
implementation-agnostic; the C++ and Rust engines realize the same work shape (the
per-pass structure verified in Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-tracecert}).

\section{Supplementary Diagnostics}

\subsection{Regression details}
\label{app:regression}
For each engine $e\in\{C,R\}$ and evaluated input, let $Y_e$ be the empirical p50
alignment time, $M$ the number of processed point--pass pairs, $S=\sumnbr$ the kernel-evaluation
count, and $Q_C=\flannevals$ and $Q_R=\kdnodes$ the engine-specific traversal counts. The
two-term model is $Y_e=a_eS+b_eQ_e+c_e$; the four-term model adds
$c_{\mathrm{pt},e}M$. Thus $a_e$, $b_e$, $c_e$, and $c_{\mathrm{pt},e}$ are fitted
kernel, traversal, intercept, and per-processed-point coefficients, respectively.
Table~\ref{tab:regression} reports both models over $n=\regN$ input observations per
engine. Regressor correlation reaches \regCorr, and wide intervals plus leave-one-out error
make coefficient-level attribution unsupported.

\subsection{Cross-ISA consistency controls}
\label{app:crossisa-controls}
The cross-ISA check uses commit-pinned fixture bytes and host counter tuples. Rust
software \texttt{libm} fixes the elementary-function implementation, fused multiply-add
contraction is disabled, and both QEMU and AArch64 hardware replay the same serialized
inputs. Agreement is checked on the complete counter tuple rather than inferred from host
results.
