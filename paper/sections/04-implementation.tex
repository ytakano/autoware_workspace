\subsection{The engines}
The C++ engine is Autoware's multigrid \ndt{}~\cite{autoware,ndtomp} (pcl-based, OpenMP;
measured serial, \texttt{num\_threads}=1). The Rust engine is a port of it that can also build
without the standard library (\texttt{no\_std}) for the bare-metal kernel deployment; behind
the C ABI it is a drop-in replacement, and the differential test suite (voxel-grid
construction, align, engine and end-to-end node paths) passes with the Rust engine substituted.
Compiler versions appear in Sec.~\ref{sec:evaluation}. \todo{pin the exact autoware\_core /
port commits at camera-ready.}

\subsection{WCET-relevant hardening of the Rust real-time path}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Zero allocation from the first frame}: all align buffers are pre-reservable
    to their worst-case sizes ($P$, \maxnn, $N_{\mathrm{iter}}{+}1$); a counting global
    allocator asserts zero allocations \emph{including} the first frame --- a growth event is
    a WCET spike, so amortized warmup is not a bound.
  \item \textbf{Recursion-free kd-tree}: the radius search runs an iterative walk over a fixed
    64-entry stack (depth $\le \lceil \log_2 N \rceil{+}1$ by the median build). The exact
    near-then-far visit order is preserved --- neighbor order feeds float summation order,
    i.e.\ bit-exactness --- and is differential-tested against the recursive oracle. The
    iterative form measured ${\approx}\oneoffKdIterSpeedup\%$ faster than the recursive one
    (same day, same core).
  \item \textbf{Panic-free, lock-free}: lint-enforced absence of panicking operations on the
    real-time path; the align path takes no locks (it reads a lock-free snapshot of the map
    state).
  \item \textbf{Zero-cost instrumentation}: counter increments are compiled out when the
    feature is disabled; the disabled build's synthetic frame-time baseline is unchanged.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Sufficiency of the neighbor cap}
The port caps the radius search at $\maxnn = 64$ where the C++ engine collects an unbounded
neighbor set --- the cap is what makes the $P \cdot \maxnn \cdot N_{\mathrm{pass}}$
decomposition exist at all. Its value is justified geometrically. A leaf's centroid lies inside
its own voxel, and with the search radius equal to the voxel size (a coupling both engines'
APIs enforce), the search ball has diameter two voxels and thus intersects at most three cells
per axis: \emph{at most $3^3 = 27$ leaves per tile can ever be in range, regardless of map
content}. Under the disjoint-tile contract, leaf multiplicity is 1, so 27 bounds the global
count and $\maxnn = 64$ carries a $2.4\times$ margin --- truncation is unreachable and
bit-exactness with the uncapped C++ engine is unconditional. (The measured maximum on a real
map is \realKmaxMax, Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-realdata}: one above the voxel-center heuristic of 8,
comfortably inside the 27.) Exceeding 64 requires at least three adversarially crowded \emph{overlapping}
tiles --- inputs that leave the comparison domain $D$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer}. There
the Rust engine truncates (its cost stays bounded) while the C++ engine's cost
grows without bound with tile multiplicity: an asymmetry we report as a structural hazard of
the uncapped implementation, not as something this analysis bounds. The equal-work assertion
doubles as the runtime membership check for $D$ (truncation breaks equal work):
\emph{dense-neighbors} sits exactly at the cap ($8 \times 8 = 64$ in-range leaves)
and passes, certifying that the two engines still saw identical neighbor sets.

\subsection{Adversarial fixture construction (engine tier)}
\label{sec:impl-fixtures}
Four hand-built generators target one hazard each. \emph{Dense-neighbors} pins $K = \maxnn =
64$ using 8 overlapping map tiles whose voxel centroids crowd shared block corners: a single
tile geometrically caps $K$ at the ${\le}8$ voxels sharing a corner when the search radius
equals the leaf size, so tile overlap --- which production dynamic map loading makes possible
in principle --- is essential to reach the cap, and this is why the frozen fixture format
stores its map as tiles. \emph{Max-iterations} is a rough random surface with
$\epsilon = 10^{-10}$ that never converges. \emph{Cache-hostile} scatters the source across a
wide map in shuffled order. \emph{Subnormal} places the source on a shell where the kernel's
$\exp$ lands in the \texttt{f64} subnormal range. The search of Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-search}
then composes and exceeds them.

\subsection{The deployment tier: preprocessing-legal worst cases}
\label{sec:impl-legal}
The fixtures above are \emph{engine-API-legal} but not \emph{preprocessing-reachable}: the
production pipeline voxel-downsamples the scan (leaf \SI{3.0}{m}; no duplicate points), caps
it at $P = 1500$ points (random downsample), and crops it to $\pm\SI{60}{m}$ --- and the map
pipeline emits disjoint, voxel-aligned tiles from a downsampled map, which caps leaf
multiplicity at 1 and hence $K$ at the per-tile geometric ceiling of 27 (Sec.~B); the
voxel-center heuristic suggests the ${\le}8$ voxels sharing a corner, but real centroids sit
anywhere inside their voxel and Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-realdata} observes $K = \realKmaxMax$
(the parameter values are
Autoware's shipped localization defaults). We therefore add a fifth fixture,
\emph{legal-worst}: the hardest such input we \emph{constructed} --- a single tile
whose per-voxel points are spaced to survive map downsampling (centroids ${\approx}$ voxel
centers, $\sqrt{3} < 2.0$ from each lattice corner), and $P = 1500$ duplicate-free source
points on distinct lattice corners at \SI{4}{m} pitch inside the crop, each collecting exactly
$K = 8$ --- the voxel-center heuristic's maximum --- with $\epsilon = 10^{-10}$ pinning the
iteration cap. Its counters certify the construction ($\bar{K} = \legalWorstKbar$ against the
engine-level 64); it is the tier's \emph{witness} --- a constructed lower bound on the tier's
worst case --- while the tier's provable ceiling remains $K \le 27$.

One axis of this fixture is deliberately \emph{not} production-parameterized: with the shipped
$\epsilon = 0.01$, its smooth lattice geometry converges in \oneoffLegalWorstProdEpsIters{}
iterations, so the fixture pins
the iteration cap through $\epsilon$ instead. The cap assumption is nevertheless not
hypothetical. Re-running the counter-guided search with the \emph{parameters frozen to the
shipped values} and every candidate checked against the preprocessing contract by an
independent legality verifier (one point per downsample cell, crop, $P$, and map-spacing
invariants) found a fully legal rough-surface geometry that oscillates to the full
\textbf{30 of 30} iterations at $\epsilon = 0.01$, frozen as \emph{legal-osc}. The production
node also ships an oscillation-count diagnostic over its per-iteration pose array ---
machinery that exists precisely because non-convergence occurs in the field (feature-poor
geometry, map mismatch, coarse initialization guesses). Within this search family the two
worst axes resist combination: reaching $K = 8$ wants a smooth lattice (which converges
quickly), while oscillation wants roughness (which scatters centroids to $\bar{K} =
\legalOscKbar$); \emph{legal-worst} ($\epsilon$-pinned, higher total cost) therefore remains
the tier's hardest measured witness and \emph{legal-osc} its iteration-axis witness.
