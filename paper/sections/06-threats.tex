\subsection{Input-space coverage}
The hill-climb explores a parameterized generator, not the full input space. The saturation
argument covers the kernel-evaluation term exactly, but the kd-traversal term has no matching
analytic maximum, so a worse traversal geometry may exist; the structural
$O(N_{\mathrm{leaves}})$ bound still caps it. On the empirical side, the real-data reference
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-realdata}) is one route, one vehicle, and a \emph{degraded-prior} guess
track (offline NDT-odometry, not the production localization filter): its iteration statistics
over-represent nominal cruising, and its margins are specific to that drive. Moreover, the
benchmark map crop limits meaningful matching to the \realOnMap{} on-map frames --- a prefix
window of the 57-minute capture --- so the effective real-data evidence base is far smaller
than the raw \realFrames{}-frame count suggests.

\subsection{Comparison validity: the divergence residual}
The real-data replay surfaced what the synthetic suites never did: iteration counts initially
diverged by ${\pm}1$ on ${\sim}2\%$ of real frames. Root-causing identified and fixed two
port-fidelity gaps invisible to translation-only fixtures --- the C++ engine transforms its
\emph{initial} pass by the guess matrix (not the Euler rebuild), and pcl's SSE transform
kernel is right-associated with the translation innermost --- reducing the divergence to
\realDivergences{} of \realFrames{} frames (\realDivPctAll\%). The honest denominator is
smaller: all \realDivergences{} divergences lie on the \realOnMap{} on-map frames
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-realdata}), i.e.\ \realOnMapDivPct\% of the frames doing meaningful
matching work. The residual is irreducible without matching glibc's
eigensolver and \texttt{f64} transcendentals bit-for-bit: sub-f32-ULP differences in the
internal \texttt{f64} trajectory (eigensolver ${\le}\oneoffEigensolverBound$ on the inverse
covariances; \texttt{libm}-vs-glibc \texttt{exp}) surface only on knife-edge frames.
``Bit-exact'' therefore means bit-exact on the tested domain: equal work is \emph{certified
per frame} by the replay assertion, and the residual is flagged rather than silently compared.
Relatedly, ISA-independence of the Rust floating-point stream is by construction
(pure-software \texttt{libm}) and now \emph{measured} on the deployment hardware
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-raspi4}): exact on \raspiOk{} of \raspiTotal{} fixtures, with one
knife-edge fixture diverging by \raspiNbrDelta{} of \raspiMismatchNbrM\,M kernel evaluations
--- deterministic, certificate-flagged rather than silent, root cause open. The work-bound
and domain-$D$ arguments are unaffected (the diverged counters remain within every enforced
cap), but absolute cross-ISA bit-exactness is a \emph{per-fixture certified} property, not
an unconditional one. The C++ comparison
itself is inherently x86/Linux-bound (pcl/ROS dependency).

provable $K$ ceiling of 27 has no constructed witness at all (legal inputs above the observed

legality verifier) plateaus at $K = \legalKSearchMax$: the legal rough-surface family
scatters centroids and cannot crowd more into one search ball, so the corner-lattice

zero-allocation guarantee is tied to the pre-reserved capacity (exceeding it costs a one-time
buffer growth).

\subsection{Evidence strength: what is enforced, checked, tested, and proven}
\label{sec:threats-epistemic}
None of the reported times is a certified hard bound, but the layers carry different epistemic
strength, and the distinction is the point of the decomposition. We distinguish four evidence
tiers: \emph{enforced by construction} (static loop guards, the hard \maxnn{} cap,
fixed-capacity buffers, the fixed-depth kd stack); \emph{statically checked} (lint-enforced
absence of panicking operations); \emph{dynamically verified} (randomized property tests, the
counting allocator, the differential suite, the per-input replay certificates); and
\emph{formally proven} --- a tier we claim for \emph{nothing} in this paper: no property has
been shown in a proof assistant, and property testing is not exhaustive. The \emph{work}
bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp} carries the strongest evidence we have --- every term enforced
by construction, with the enforcement dynamically verified over randomized inputs --- and for
the kernel-evaluation term a frozen input demonstrably \emph{achieves} the maximum (the
saturation certificate, an exact counter identity; the kd-traversal term
has no matching certificate, so a worse traversal geometry may exist within its structural
cap). Every \emph{time} figure, by contrast, is an observation: warm caches, one container,
no interference, and a microarchitecture (out-of-order x86) for which no sound static timing
model exists --- on this hardware class a true WCET is practically unobtainable by any method,
and the Gumbel extrapolation inherits MBPTA's representativity caveats. The counter
decomposition thus \emph{factorizes} WCET into a structurally-enforced part (work) and an
empirical part
(time per unit of work); only the former is load-bearing. On a timing-predictable target ---
precisely the \texttt{no\_std} kernel deployment of Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} --- a defensible
per-operation cost bound would promote $\text{work bound} \times \text{unit-cost bound}$ to
an analytical WCET. Separately, the structural-hazard removals (allocation-, recursion-,
panic-freedom) are constructive properties, not measurements: they are what make a finite
bound \emph{possible} at all --- an engine that calls the allocator inside its align loop
forfeits the premise of a finite WCET under fragmentation. The deliverable is therefore ``the
port does not regress WCET on any adversarial fixture, removes the structural hazards, and
carries a work envelope enforced by construction and machine-checked'' --- not a proven
absolute time bound.
