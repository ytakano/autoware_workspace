\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}


\subsection{Measurement and statistical rigor}
Engine-tier timing follows the controlled Profile-B protocol (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-setup}):
isolated pinned core, \envSessions{} sessions spanning a reboot cycle with randomized
orders, warm/cold/co-runner series separated, and a calibration guard that aborted (and
thereby excluded) two transient firmware-level frequency clamps it caught mid-campaign. On
\texttt{nohz\_full} cores the kernel's frequency reporting is stale, so measured sustained
speed is the only reliable throttling detector. Cross-session stability bounds the residual
environment sensitivity: per-session medians agree within \sessMedSpreadWorstPct\% (worst
fixture; a per-session memory-layout effect that persists with or without a reboot), and
the Rust/C++ max ratios are stable to $\pm\sessRatioSpreadWorst$.

Remaining limits: one machine; the real-data replay of Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-realdata} is
single-shot (one align per frame per engine under Profile A), so its per-frame times carry
no repetition statistics; the parallel-feasibility numbers of
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-parallel} are a host throughput observation, not a multi-core WCET result
(worker interference and scheduling under contention are unmodeled, and the serial engine
stays the deterministic baseline); and the full POT/MLE
extreme-value treatment was run and its \emph{diagnostics rejected extrapolation}. The
per-session sample series carry serial dependence up to $|\mathrm{ACF}| = \evtAcfWorst$, so
no extrapolated exceedance probability is claimed, and the tail deliverable is the
empirical maxima with cross-session stability (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-evt}).

\subsection{Input-space coverage}
The hill-climb explores a parameterized generator, not the full input space. The saturation
argument covers the kernel-evaluation term exactly, but the kd-traversal term has no matching
analytic maximum, so a worse traversal geometry may exist; the structural
$O(N_{\mathrm{leaves}})$ bound still caps it. On the empirical side, the real-data reference
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-realdata}) is one route, one vehicle, and a \emph{degraded-prior} guess
track (offline NDT-odometry, not the production localization filter): its iteration statistics
do not represent nominal cruising, and its margins are specific to that drive. Moreover, the
benchmark map crop limits meaningful matching to the \realOnMap{} on-map frames, a prefix
window of the 57-minute capture, so the effective real-data evidence base is far smaller
than the raw \realFrames{}-frame count suggests.

\subsection{Comparison validity: the divergence residual}
The real-data replay surfaced a case absent from the synthetic suites: iteration counts initially
diverged by ${\pm}1$ on ${\sim}2\%$ of real frames. Root-causing identified and fixed two
port-fidelity gaps invisible to translation-only fixtures: the C++ engine transforms its
\emph{initial} pass by the guess matrix (not the Euler rebuild), and pcl's SSE transform
kernel is right-associated with the translation innermost. Fixing both reduced the
divergence to \realDivergences{} of \realFrames{} frames (\realDivPctAll\%). The honest
denominator is smaller: all \realDivergences{} divergences lie on the \realOnMap{} on-map
frames (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-realdata}), i.e.\ \realOnMapDivPct\% of the frames doing
meaningful matching work.

The residual is irreducible without matching glibc's eigensolver and \texttt{f64}
transcendentals bit-for-bit (eigensolver ${\le}\oneoffEigensolverBound$ on the inverse
covariances, and \texttt{libm} against glibc \texttt{exp}). More fundamentally, it is
irreducible without matching the per-point neighbor accumulation \emph{order}, which differs
by design: pcl/FLANN returns distance-sorted neighbors whereas the port returns them in
kd-visit order (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-tracecert}). These sub-f32-ULP \texttt{f64} differences
surface only on the marginal frames, which is
why the paper claims work equivalence only over the certified subset $E$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer}): all \realDivergences{} divergent frames lie \emph{inside}
the bounded-neighbor domain $D$, so membership in $D$ alone does not guarantee equal work;
equal work is \emph{certified per frame}, and the residual is flagged rather than silently
compared. The trace certificate shows the same mechanism operating below the iteration
threshold: \traceRealWorkGapFrames{} equal-iteration frames differ in their per-point
neighbor sets by up to \traceRealWorkGapRelMaxPct\% of their kernel work
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-tracecert}), which is why cross-language work statements are scoped to
trace-certified inputs.

Relatedly, the Rust floating-point stream is \emph{designed} for ISA-stable execution
(pure-software \texttt{libm}, no FMA contraction in the kernel image) and \emph{verified}
on the deployment-class evaluation target
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-raspi4}): the counters match exactly on all \raspiTotal{} fixtures. The one flag the
on-target certificate initially raised was root-caused to a stale expected-value snapshot
predating one of the port-fidelity fixes above, a reminder that the counters are sensitive
to the engine \emph{version} at parts-per-million resolution: cross-version baselines must
be commit-pinned, and cross-ISA equality (bit-identical counters) remains a per-fixture
\emph{certified} property rather than an assumed one. The C++ comparison itself is
inherently x86/Linux-bound (pcl/ROS dependency).

\subsection{Safety and verification scope}
Our rationale for the Rust port is a reduction in verification surface, not a claim that
Rust is necessary for bounded execution. A C++ redesign could impose the same allocation,
recursion, loop, and neighbor bounds. Within the safe Rust core, however, ownership,
lifetime, and aliasing rules discharge memory-safety and data-race-freedom obligations that
would otherwise require project-specific evidence. These language guarantees do not cover
the C ABI and FFI boundary, any \texttt{unsafe} implementation used behind a safe interface,
or trusted dependencies; all remain part of the verification surface. Likewise,
\texttt{no\_std} removes a deployment dependency on the Rust standard library but does not
by itself provide bounded execution or real-time behavior. Those properties depend on the
explicit hardening and contracts of Sec.~\ref{sec:implementation}.

Table~\ref{tab:preconditions} collects every precondition of the work
bound~\eqref{eq:decomp}: its limit, who enforces it, and what happens on violation,
distinguishing limits the engine enforces at runtime from contracts that are merely
\emph{assumed}. The engine-tier fixtures are API-legal but not reachable through the
production preprocessing (duplicate source points, sub-downsample map clusters, overlapping
tiles); the bound they support is the bounded-neighbor engine tier over $D$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer}), deliberately conservative.

The deployment tier's contracts (tile disjointness and alignment, map/scan downsampling,
the $P \le 1500$ cap) are properties of the map and sensing pipelines that the engine does
\emph{not} verify; a violated contract silently moves the system back to the engine tier.
The tier is therefore an \emph{assumption}, not a guarantee; converting it into one
requires a boundary validator that checks the assumed rows of
Table~\ref{tab:preconditions} at runtime with a defined rejection behavior (future work,
with its overhead measured).

On the iteration axis, the tier's time witness (\emph{legal-worst}) pins
$N_{\mathrm{iter}} = 30$ via a non-production $\epsilon$. The cap itself is legally
reachable (the production-parameterized \emph{legal-osc} witness,
Sec.~\ref{sec:impl-legal}), but no single legal input in our search family achieves the
$K = 8$ and 30-iteration worst cases \emph{simultaneously}: the tier bound combines the two
axes conservatively rather than exhibiting one witness for both. The tier's provable $K$
ceiling of 27 has no constructed witness at all (legal inputs above the observed
$K = \realKmaxMax$ may exist below it). A dedicated production-parameterized search with
fitness set to the per-point maximum $K$ (\legalKSearchSeeds{} seeds through the same
legality verifier) plateaus at $K = \legalKSearchMax$: the legal rough-surface family
scatters centroids and cannot crowd more into one search ball, so the corner-lattice
construction ($K = 8$) and the real map ($K = \realKmaxMax$) remain the strongest known
legal witnesses. Within these searched families, this is evidence that legal $K$ concentrates far below the ceiling, though
not a proof.

Two further preconditions: the \maxnn{} sufficiency derivation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:implementation}) assumes the search radius stays coupled to the voxel size
(decoupling them would require re-deriving the cap), and the affine
$T_{\mathrm{obs,max}}(P)$ envelope is validated over $P \in [\psweepPmin{}, \psweepPmax{}]$
on the host
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-psweep}); beyond that range or on other platforms, working sets exceeding the last-level
cache could bend the slope upward, and the zero-allocation guarantee is tied to the
pre-reserved capacity (exceeding it costs a one-time buffer growth).

\subsection{Evidence strength: what is enforced, checked, tested, and proven}
\label{sec:threats-epistemic}
None of the reported times is a certified hard bound, but the layers carry different
epistemic strength, and the distinction is the point of the decomposition. We distinguish
four evidence tiers: \emph{enforced by construction} (static loop guards, the hard \maxnn{}
cap, fixed-capacity buffers, the fixed-depth kd stack); \emph{statically checked}
(lint-enforced absence of panicking operations); \emph{dynamically verified} (randomized
property tests, the counting allocator, the differential suite, the per-input replay
certificates); and \emph{formally proven}, a tier we claim for \emph{nothing} in this
paper: no property has been shown in a proof assistant, and property testing is not
exhaustive.

The \emph{work} bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp} carries the strongest evidence we have:
every term enforced by construction, with the enforcement dynamically verified over
randomized inputs, and for the kernel-evaluation term a frozen input demonstrably
\emph{achieves} the maximum (the saturation certificate, an exact counter identity). The
kd-traversal term has no matching certificate, so a worse traversal geometry may exist
within its structural cap; the frontier ablation of Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-search} shows this
caveat binds, since on Rust a kd-heavier frontier member runs
$\ablationFrontierRustExcessPct\%$ past the lexicographic champion.

Every \emph{time} figure, by contrast, is an observation: one machine, one protocol
(controlled and interference-probed, but still measured), and a microarchitecture
(out-of-order x86) for which no sound static timing model exists. On this hardware class a
true WCET is practically unobtainable by any method, and extreme-value extrapolation failed
its own diagnostics here (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-evt}). The bridge experiment adds a sharper
warning about the measured part: the \emph{controlled configuration itself} is associated
with a \SI{\bridgeCppTaxMinMs}{}--\SI{\bridgeCppTaxMaxMs}{ms} shift in one engine's
per-align constant (mechanism unresolved; Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-bridge}), so
controlled numbers are configuration-specific observations, not platform-free truths.

In this case study, the counter decomposition \emph{factorizes} WCET evidence into a structurally-enforced part
(work) and an empirical part (time per unit of work); only the former carries the strong
evidence tier. On a timing-predictable target, precisely the \texttt{no\_std} kernel
deployment of Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}, a defensible per-operation cost bound would promote
$\text{work bound} \times \text{unit-cost bound}$ to an analytical WCET. Separately, the
structural-hazard removals (allocation-, recursion-, panic-freedom) are constructive
properties, not measurements: they are what make a finite bound \emph{possible} at all,
since an engine that calls the allocator inside its align loop cannot be given a defensible
finite bound under this allocator/system model. The deliverable is therefore ``the port
does not regress WCET on any adversarial fixture, removes the structural hazards, and
carries a work envelope enforced by construction and checked by property tests,'' not a
proven absolute time bound.
