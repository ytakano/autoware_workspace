\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\subsection{Measurement and coverage}
Isolated timing uses a pinned core, \envSessions{} randomized sessions spanning a reboot,
separate warm/cold/co-runner series, and a calibration guard that rejected two transient
frequency clamps. Worst-fixture session medians agree within \sessMedSpreadWorstPct\%, and
Rust/C++ max ratios within $\pm\sessRatioSpreadWorst$. Nevertheless, all timing comes from
one machine. The campaign compares complete deployable implementations under recorded
release settings, not languages in isolation; compiler flags have no exact cross-toolchain
mapping, and allocators, layouts, kd-trees, libraries, and compiler choices all contribute.
Real replay is single-shot per frame, and parallel results omit worker scheduling and
interference, so neither supports a multi-core WCET claim.

Search covers a parameterized generator, not all inputs. Kernel-evaluation saturation is
exact, but the traversal objective is the Rust-specific kd-node count and has only its
$O(N_{\mathrm{leaves}})$ structural cap; a slower Rust geometry may exist. No independent
search uses the C++ FLANN distance or plane-evaluation counters, so C++ timing evidence is
limited to observations over the transferred fixture set. The real-data evidence covers one
vehicle, route, and cropped map, with
only \realOnMap{} on-map frames in \realSegments{} short segments. The remaining
\realOffMap{} frames are near-no-op aligns and therefore do not characterize localization
work over the full recording. The degraded-prior track uses offline NDT odometry rather than
the production filter, so it does not represent nominal cruising or closed-loop behavior.
The NDT--EKF replay includes feedback but lacks ground truth, vehicle dynamics, and
co-resident workloads. Same-engine repetitions also diverge, preventing whole-drive
language attribution; environment-gated EKF tracing may itself perturb scheduling.

\subsection{Cross-language and cross-ISA validity}
Two corrected port gaps reduced real-frame iteration-count divergence to
\realIterationDivergent{} of \realFrames{} frames, all among the \realOnMap{} on-map set
(\realOnMapDivPct\%). The stricter $E_{\mathrm{base}}$ criterion excludes
\realBaseDivergent{} on-map frames: \realOutputDivergent{} have equal iterations but exceed
at least one output threshold, and \realIterationDivergent{} take different iteration
paths. Residual differences are consistent with glibc versus \texttt{libm}, eigensolver
behavior, and neighbor accumulation order.

Work-trace claims apply only to $E_{\mathrm{shape}}$; \realBaseOnly{} base-only frames
still differ by up to \traceRealWorkGapRelMaxPct\% in kernel evaluations. The canonical
SHA-256 shape digest records leaf identity but not leaf parameters. The separate payload
digest includes means and regularized inverse covariances, yet agrees on only
\tracePayloadExactFixtures{} synthetic fixtures and \tracePayloadExactOnMap{} on-map
frames. Consequently, neither digest establishes full semantic equivalence. SHA-256
collisions remain computationally possible. The C++ trace records complete uncapped
neighbor lists, so shape agreement confirms at most \maxnn{} leaves at the C++ query
coordinates. The current Rust search detects a 65th leaf at its own coordinate; the
post-change \runtimeValidationFrames{}-frame replay triggered no such diagnostic. This
does not remove the coordinate difference or retroactively add the diagnostic to the
cross-language trace relation.

The Rust stream targets ISA stability through software \texttt{libm} and no FMA contraction;
all \raspiTotal{} frozen fixtures match on the AArch64 target. This is a per-fixture check,
not a general ISA proof. The initial stale-baseline mismatch also shows that expected
counters must be commit-pinned. The C++ leg remains x86/Linux-specific because it depends
on PCL and ROS.

\subsection{Safety and contract scope}
Rust reduces the verification surface but is not required for bounded execution; an
appropriately redesigned C++ engine could impose the same loop, allocation, recursion, and
neighbor bounds. Safe Rust discharges memory-safety and data-race-freedom obligations, but
the C ABI, unsafe internals, and trusted dependencies remain in scope. Likewise,
\texttt{no\_std} removes a library dependency rather than guaranteeing real-time behavior.

Engine-configuration stress fixtures are API-legal but need not satisfy production

No searched contract-conforming input combines $K=8$ with 30 shipped-parameter iterations:
\emph{geom-stress} pins the latter through non-shipped $\epsilon$, while
\emph{shipped-osc} establishes a cap-reaching witness at shipped settings. The analytic $K\le27$
ceiling has no witness and assumes boundary and floating-point behavior. A
\legalKSearchSeeds{}-seed contract-conforming search reaches only
$K=\legalKSearchMax$; this is evidence, not proof, that reachable $K$ stays below the
ceiling. The affine $P$ fit is validated only
for $P\in[\psweepPmin{},\psweepPmax{}]$ on the host, and allocation freedom holds only
inside pre-reserved capacities. Allocation freedom removes one kernel-level latency path;
it does not bound interrupts, system-management interrupts, page faults, scheduler delays,
or memory-hierarchy interference on the Linux host.

\subsection{Evidence strength}
\label{sec:threats-epistemic}
Loop guards, the neighbor cap, reserved buffers, and the fixed-depth stack are enforced by
construction. Linting excludes explicit panic paths in project-owned code, while property,
allocation, differential, and replay tests provide dynamic evidence. We claim no
machine-checked formal proof. The Rust envelope's maximum for the common
kernel-evaluation term has an exact saturation witness, whereas neither a Rust traversal
maximum nor a C++ traversal extremum is established.

All times are platform-specific observations. Tail extrapolation is withheld because the
diagnostic finds serial dependence reaching $|\mathrm{ACF}|=\evtAcfWorst$. Separately, the
bridge experiment associates
the controlled configuration with a \SI{\bridgeCppTaxMinMs}--\SI{\bridgeCppTaxMaxMs}{ms}
unresolved C++ shift. The result is therefore a structurally enforced and property-tested
work envelope plus observed maxima, not a certified hard timing bound. On a predictable
target, a defensible per-operation bound could combine with the work envelope to establish
an analytical WCET.
