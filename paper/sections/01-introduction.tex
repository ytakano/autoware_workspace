\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autoware~\cite{autoware} localizes a vehicle by aligning each LiDAR scan to a Gaussian
voxel map with the Normal Distributions Transform (\ndt)~\cite{biber2003ndt}. At 10\,Hz,
one align has a \SI{100}{ms} period before a late pose forces downstream estimation toward
dead reckoning. Yet input geometry changes Newton iterations, neighbor multiplicity, and
kd-tree traversal: legal synthetic inputs span under \SI{10}{ms} to nearly one second on
the same core. Nominal replay therefore cannot answer whether the deadline has defensible
margin.

Classical static WCET analysis struggles because the dominant bounds are input properties;
exhaustive enumeration is infeasible, while wall-clock-guided fuzzing is noisy and
machine-specific. We instead separate a structural work envelope from measured
platform cost, then search deterministic work counters for stress witnesses.

Rust is useful here not because C++ cannot be bounded, but because ownership, lifetimes, and
aliasing rules discharge memory-safety and data-race-freedom obligations in the safe
core~\cite{jung2018rustbelt}. This narrows project-specific validation to equivalence,
numerics, work bounds, and timing. The reduction excludes the FFI, unsafe code, and trusted
dependencies. The port also builds as \texttt{no\_std} for real-time environments without
the standard library. Its radius query caps neighbors at \maxnn; the
\emph{bounded-neighbor domain} $D$ contains inputs whose uncapped queries already satisfy
that limit. Per-input traces compare a defined work shape on evaluated inputs; leaf payloads,
traversal, memory operations, and latency are assessed separately.

The objective is to realize a real-time-oriented NDT engine through the Rust port, with
explicit work limits and timing evidence for its critical path. The production C++ engine
serves as the behavioral and timing baseline for the port. We do not attempt to search or
establish the C++ implementation's worst traversal shape.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A three-layer analysis combining a property-tested structural work bound,
    deterministic counter-guided input search, and controlled timing with diagnostics-gated
    MBPTA. Failed diagnostics prohibit extrapolation.
  \item A per-input cross-language work-shape conformance check. The base-agreement set
    $E_{\mathrm{base}}$ checks iteration,
    pose, and score tolerances; $E_{\mathrm{shape}}$ additionally checks the recorded
    pass structure, point and neighbor counts, and canonical leaf identities. A separate
    payload digest checks leaf means and inverse covariances. Neither establishes runtime
    membership in $D$ or full semantic equivalence.
  \item Search evidence that reaches
    $P\maxnn(N_{\mathrm{iter}}{+}1)$ kernel evaluations and then raises the Rust
    implementation's kd-node count by \searchKdGrowthPct\%, with frozen Pareto alternatives
    timed separately.
  \item An industrial case study in which Rust removes the C++ per-point allocation hazard
    within reserved capacities. Across the primary and counter-Pareto frontier campaigns,
    its engine-specific observed maximum is \SI{\combinedFixtureMaxRust}{ms}, versus
    \SI{\combinedFixtureMaxCpp}{ms} for C++. A two-tier work envelope has deployment
    axes backed by legal witnesses, while one-drive replay exposes the residual conformance
    boundary.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:problem}
formulates the WCET problem and its structural work decomposition.
Sections~\ref{sec:methodology} and~\ref{sec:implementation} present the three-layer
method, cross-language work-shape conformance check, and implementation, while
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} reports the search, timing, trace, real-data, and cross-ISA
results. Sections~\ref{sec:threats} and~\ref{sec:related} discuss limitations and prior
work, respectively; Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} presents the conclusions, and the appendix provides
detailed regression, EVT, and cross-ISA diagnostics.
