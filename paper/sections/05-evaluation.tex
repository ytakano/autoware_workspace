\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}
We use three named measurement configurations. \emph{Isolated} denotes controlled engine
characterization on a pinned core, \emph{Replay} denotes real-data replay under normal CFS
scheduling, and \emph{Bare-metal} denotes evaluation on the AArch64 \texttt{no\_std} target.
All engine-tier timing follows the Isolated configuration of our measurement policy. The
host uses \envCpu{} and \envKernel{}. The benchmark core is
isolated with \texttt{isolcpus} and \texttt{nohz\_full}; IRQ affinities are moved, with
three immovable IRQs remaining. Its SMT sibling is offline, and the \envGovernor{}
governor pins its frequency at \SI{3.2}{GHz}. We compile C++ with \envCppCompiler{} using
\texttt{CMAKE\_BUILD\_TYPE=Release} and \envCppReleaseFlags. Rust uses
\envRustCompiler{} with Cargo release defaults \texttt{opt-level=3},
\texttt{lto=false}, and \texttt{codegen-units=16}, plus the workspace override
\texttt{overflow-checks=true}. No target CPU is explicitly selected, and the manifests
record no experiment-specific \texttt{RUSTFLAGS}.

The campaign comprises \envSessions{} sessions on \envDate{}, including one session
across a reboot cycle, with randomized fixture and engine order. For each fixture and
engine, every session records \envItersWarmPerSession{} warm and
\envItersTailPerSession{} tail samples after \envWarmup{} warmups. We pool these samples
across sessions for the warm series; Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-interference} reports the cold and
co-runner series. A \texttt{steady\_clock} measures only the align loop, after the map and
kd-tree have been built once per engine and fixture. A fixed-work calibration guard aborts any series whose
sustained speed shifts by more than 10\%; it fired twice on transient firmware-level
clamps, which were excluded by re-running the affected series. Engines were measured at
autoware\_core commit \texttt{\envCommit}. The released data files embed per-run
manifests containing the environment, compiler flags, commits, and fixture and binary hashes.

\subsection{Search effectiveness}
\label{sec:eval-search}
The frozen hill-climb champion saturates the analytic kernel-evaluation maximum:
$\sumnbr = P \cdot \maxnn \cdot (N_{\mathrm{iter}}{+}1) = 2000 \cdot 64 \cdot 31 =
\num{3968000}$, certifying that the hand-built \emph{dense-neighbors} construction is
kernel-count-optimal. It then grows the remaining free term \kdnodes{} by a further
\searchKdGrowthPct\%, from \oneoffKdGenZeroM\,M to \searchZeroKdM\,M, while increasing
the map extent from 6 to 9 blocks. The generation-0 value comes from the recorded search
log and the final value from the frozen fixture's counters.
The shared counter-extremal witness is \emph{search-00}. It is slower than every other frozen fixture
on every engine and percentile in Table~\ref{tab:tails}; the frontier ablation below refines
the scope of that statement. Table~\ref{tab:counters} lists the frozen set.

The full common-work trace certificate passes on every fixture. The base diagnostics also

The largest score delta is \certMaxScoreDelta{} on \emph{subnormal}, or one \texttt{f32} ULP.
The comparison therefore uses trace-certified common semantic work. The Rust port's observed maximum is at or below
the C++ engine's on the measured fixtures in Table~\ref{tab:tails}. Ratios span
\tailsRatioMin--\tailsRatioMax, and the union worst falls from
\SI{\tailsSearchMaxCpp}{ms} to \SI{\tailsSearchMaxRust}{ms}.

The \emph{subnormal} fixture's outlier ratio of $\subnormalMedianRatio\times$ at p50 required
a controlled check. Re-generating the same fixture with the source shell pulled from
\SI{1.99}{m} to \SI{1.69}{m}, which moves every kernel $\exp$ result out of the subnormal

the control is marginally \emph{slower}. C++ p50 rises from
\SI{\oneoffShellSubCpp}{ms} to \SI{\oneoffShellCtrlCpp}{ms}, and Rust p50 rises from
\oneoffShellSubRust{} to \oneoffShellCtrlRust. On this microarchitecture, subnormal
operands are therefore \emph{not} the mechanism on this fixture; if anything, removing
them costs slightly more. A
microbenchmark confirms that subnormal-result \texttt{exp} costs only
$\oneoffExpSubGlibc\times$/$\oneoffExpSubLibm\times$ per call for glibc/\texttt{libm},
negligible at this fixture's $\bar{K}=\subnormalKbar$.

The real driver is the \emph{per-point constant overhead} exposed by a neighbor-sparse
shape: with almost no kernel work, C++ spends
${\approx}\subnormalPtPassCppUs$\,\si{\micro\second} per point per pass against Rust's
$\subnormalPtPassRustUs$, an ${\approx}\ptPassGapUs$\,\si{\micro\second} gap that reappears
as the same constant on \emph{cache-hostile}, where
$\bar{K}=\cacheHostileKbar$. The measured allocator fast path costs
\SI{\oneoffMallocPairNs}{ns} per malloc/free pair across approximately
\subnormalAllocPairs{} pairs and accounts for only
${\sim}\subnormalAllocSharePct\%$. The remainder is the per-query search machinery
around pcl/FLANN, including call indirection and result-container handling, that the Rust engine's direct,
allocation-free kd query does not pay. This refines the allocation analysis of
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-alloc}: heap churn is inexpensive \emph{on average}, and its significance is
the difficulty of establishing a finite latency bound under this allocator/system model,
while the mean-cost gap on sparse inputs is search-machinery
overhead. Hardware performance counters are unavailable in the measurement container, so
the final split is arithmetic over microbenchmarks rather than counter-attributed.
Section~\ref{sec:threats} records this limitation.

\subsection{Per-input trace certificate}
\label{sec:eval-tracecert}
\input{tables/tracecert}

The trace certificate of Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer} was executed on every frozen fixture
and on all \realFrames{} captured real frames. The traced analysis build of the C++ engine
uses an instrumented copy of the engine sources plus a counting FLANN distance functor;
the shipped sources remain byte-identical. It emits a per-pass trace that the replay compares leg
by leg against the Rust engine's mirrored trace. Every quantity is a deterministic function
of the input, so the runs need no controlled environment, and the traced binaries are never
used for timing.

Table~\ref{tab:tracecert} shows the fixture leg. The \emph{structural} legs comprise the
pass count, per-pass point and neighbor counts, and each point's FNV-1a hash over
neighbor-leaf mean bits. Counts and hashes agree on all \traceFixtures{} inputs, the
line-search loop is never entered, and the measured C++ pass count equals
$N_{\mathrm{iter}}{+}1$ on every input: the common-work
transfer previously certified indirectly through iteration, pose, score, and the allocation
leg of Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-alloc} is now measured directly on the C++ side, per input. The
\emph{numeric} legs quantify the fidelity: the per-pass score agrees to
\traceUlpConvMax{} f64 ULP or better on the well-conditioned fixtures and is bit-identical
on \emph{cache-hostile}, but drifts to $\traceUlpWorstTex$ ULP on the most ill-conditioned geometries. The
mechanism is structural, not a defect: pcl/FLANN returns each point's neighbors sorted by
distance while the port's kd walk returns them in visit order, so the two engines
accumulate the count/hash-matched per-point neighbor sets in different orders; f64 bit-equality of the
sums is unattainable by design, and equivalence is correctly claimed at the \emph{work}
level. The hash has a non-zero collision probability, so this is not a formal proof of
literal set equality.

The real-data leg turns the necessary-vs-sufficient distinction into measurement. The
traced replay reproduces the frozen capture frame-exactly, with identical per-frame
iteration counts and match verdicts on all \realFrames{} frames. Of the
\realBaseOnMap{} on-map frames in $E_{\mathrm{base}}$, \realTraceOnMap{} also
belong to $E_{\mathrm{trace}}$, while \traceRealWorkGapFrames{} perform
\emph{equal-iteration but unequal common semantic work}: their point-neighbor evaluations
differ by a median
$|\Delta\sumnbr|$ of \traceRealWorkGapNbrMed{} and a maximum of \traceRealWorkGapNbrMax,
at most \traceRealWorkGapRelMaxPct\% of the frame's kernel evaluations. This is the same
boundary mechanism as the iteration divergences, caught here below the iteration threshold.
In this replay, equal iteration count is therefore measurably \emph{not} sufficient for
common-work equivalence. The trace-certified set contains \realTraceAll{} of
\realFrames{} frames overall and \realTraceOnMap{} of \realOnMap{} on-map frames. The
\realIterationDivergent{}
iteration-divergent frames all fork late, between passes
\traceRealDivFirstMin and \traceRealDivFirstMax, with median \traceRealDivFirstMed. They
then take different convergence paths, with iteration counts up to
$\pm\traceRealDivIterDeltaMax$ apart and final poses up to
\SI{\traceRealDivergentTransDeltaMax}{m} apart. Every fixture-level comparison
in this paper carries the full trace certificate; real-data work statements are scoped to
$E_{\mathrm{trace}}$.

\subsection{Heap allocations per align}
\label{sec:eval-alloc}
The interposer counts decompose as ${\approx}\allocPerPtPass$ allocations per point per
derivative pass on every fixture. For example,
$\allocWorstPerAlign{} / (31 \cdot 2000) = \allocPerPtPassExact$. This locates the
source in the per-point inner loop: a fresh neighbor
vector constructed inside the parallel loop body, two fresh index/distance vectors per
radius-search call, and approximately 8 per-query result containers per point inside pcl/FLANN.
These ride the allocator's thread-cache fast path, invisible on average, but each is a
potential lock, page fault, or fragmentation stall inside the innermost loop. The Rust
engine hoists all three allocation sites into a pre-reserved workspace and measures zero in
the same harness, consistent with the counting-allocator-verified allocation freedom shown
in Table~\ref{tab:alloc}.

Beyond the hazard reading, these counts double as the \emph{independent work certificate
for the C++ side} defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer}. The numerator comes from the
untouched production binary via \texttt{LD\_PRELOAD}, while the denominator
$P \cdot N_{\mathrm{pass}}$ comes from the Rust counters. The ratio is
\allocCertMin--\allocCertMax{} on every fixture
whose radius queries return neighbors. This is a \allocCertSpreadPct\% spread across
geometries whose cost differs by two orders of magnitude, fine enough that a single extra
C++ derivative pass, which would shift the count by about 3\% at
$N_{\mathrm{pass}} = 31$, could not hide. The
one deviation, \emph{subnormal} at \allocCertSparse{}, is itself structural: its
mostly-empty radius searches skip the per-result containers.

\subsection{Cache and interference sensitivity}
\label{sec:eval-interference}
\input{tables/interference}

Table~\ref{tab:interference} reports two further Isolated series that probe the
warm-series results. The \emph{cold} series uses between-sample software cache eviction
and shifts medians by at most \coldWorstPct\% across the whole fixture set: the
worst-case-shaped inputs are already cache-hostile, so eviction adds little. The
\emph{co-runner} series stresses one hardware resource at a time from an L3-sharing core
and splits cleanly along the two-tier structure. On \emph{search-00}, whose cost is
kernel-evaluation-bound and cache-resident, no co-runner moves the maximum by more than
${\sim}\coWorstSearch\%$. On \emph{legal-worst}, whose cost is kd-traversal/memory-bound
as described in Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-twotier}, the memory-bandwidth co-runner inflates the
Rust observed maximum by up to $+\coWorstLegal\%$, while the LLC and FP co-runners remain null. The
deployment tier, not the engine-tier worst, is thus the interference-sensitive one, which
is what its kd-dominated cost structure predicts.

\subsection{Unit-cost regression}
\label{sec:eval-regression}
Regression is diagnostic, not unit-cost identification. Across the fixtures and $P$-sweep

adopted Rust model has a \regLooMaxRust\% worst leave-one-out error. Moreover, each engine

direct fixture maxima in Table~\ref{tab:tails}, not coefficient attribution; the models,

\subsection{Scaling with the input size $P$}
\label{sec:eval-psweep}
In the decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp}, $P$ is the only multiplicative freedom: it scales the
per-iteration sum while leaving $N_{\mathrm{iter}}$, $K$, and $T_{\mathrm{solve}}$ untouched,
predicting an \emph{affine} observed envelope $T_{\mathrm{obs,max}}(P) =
\mathrm{slope}\cdot P + \mathrm{const}$. This is a fit to observed maxima, not a hard bound.
We check this prediction with a controlled sweep: the shared counter-extremal geometry regenerated at $P \in
[\psweepPmin{}, \psweepPmax{}]$ in six points with $\times 2$ steps. The counters certify
that per-point work is exactly invariant: $N_{\mathrm{iter}} = 30$ and
$\sumnbr = P \cdot 64 \cdot 31$ at every $P$. Time may therefore vary only through $P$ itself.
Table~\ref{tab:psweep} and Fig.~\ref{fig:psweep} show the result: both engines are affine in
$P$ on the \emph{max} series, with $R^2 = \psweepRsqCpp{}$ for C++ and
$\psweepRsqRust{}$ for Rust;
no cache knee is visible over the tested range. The fitted observed-maximum slope is
\SI{\psweepSlopeCpp}{\micro\second} for C++ versus
\SI{\psweepSlopeRust}{\micro\second} for Rust
per source point, consistent with the unit costs of
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-regression}: approximately 64 kernel evaluations across 31 passes per
point, plus the per-point and traversal terms.

Within the tested range, the affine envelope makes the result \emph{parametric in the caller's contract}: inverting it
against the 10\,Hz, \SI{100}{ms} budget gives a maximum admissible downsampled-scan size of
$P \approx \psweepPmaxRust{}$ points for the Rust engine and \psweepPmaxCpp{} for C++
\emph{under the shared counter-extremal geometry on this host}, a number the localization node's
voxel-filter configuration can be audited against directly. These values are host-relative;
the same protocol yields the relevant slope on deployment hardware.

\subsection{Two-tier work-envelope analysis}
\label{sec:eval-twotier}
The adversarial fixtures are constructed stress witnesses for the engine API over the
bounded-neighbor domain $D$; they are not upper bounds on that domain.
This is the bounded-neighbor engine tier defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer} and the relevant contract for
the \texttt{no\_std} kernel deployment, where inputs cross a trust boundary unvalidated. When the production
preprocessing contracts of Sec.~\ref{sec:impl-legal} hold, the reachable input space
shrinks. On \emph{legal-worst}, the hardest
preprocessing-reachable input we constructed, the observed maximum drops to
\SI{\legalWorstMaxRust}{ms} for Rust and \SI{\legalWorstMaxCpp}{ms} for C++. These are
measured factors of \legalWorstRatioRust$\times$ and \legalWorstRatioCpp$\times$ below
the shared counter-extremal witness in Table~\ref{tab:tails}. These are measured
witness-to-witness gaps.

Two caveats scope this factor. It is a \emph{fixture-to-fixture measurement}, not a proven
tightening: \emph{legal-worst} realizes the voxel-center heuristic $K = 8$ per point, while
the tier's analytic ceiling is $K \le 27$ under the assumptions of
Sec.~\ref{sec:implementation}.
The tightening
the contracts alone guarantee on the kernel term is the parametric $64/27 \approx
2.4\times$, and a legal input between our witness and that ceiling may exist. And it is
conditional on the contracts actually holding because the engine does not check them.
Table~\ref{tab:preconditions} lists these assumptions.

The mechanism is the $K$ ceiling: disjoint voxel-aligned map tiles cap leaf multiplicity at
1, capping $K$ at 27 instead of 64. This witness realizes
$\bar{K} = \legalWorstKbar$, and the production random-downsample cap fixes
$P \le 1500$. Note the shift in the cost
structure: kernel evaluations drop $\twoTierKernelDrop\times$, with $\sumnbr$ falling
from $\searchZeroSumNbrM\,\mathrm{M}$ to $\legalWorstSumNbrK\,\mathrm{k}$, but time
drops less, because kd-tree traversal, unaffected by the $K$ ceiling, becomes the dominant
term of the deployment tier. For these witnesses, the pair quantifies what \emph{enforcing
input contracts at the component boundary} buys: validating tile disjointness and
scan-downsample invariants before the align yields a measured observed-maximum gap of
$\legalWorstRatioRust\times$. The kernel term improves by a provable factor of at least
$2.4\times$, without touching
the kernel itself. The iteration axis of the tier is not an artifact of the $\epsilon$ pin:
the production-parameterized legal search of Sec.~\ref{sec:impl-legal} reaches the full
30-iteration cap on \emph{legal-osc}. It costs \SI{\legalOscMaxRust}{ms} in Rust and
\SI{\legalOscMaxCpp}{ms} in C++, with $\bar{K} = \legalOscKbar$. This remains below
\emph{legal-worst}, which
therefore remains the tier's hardest measured witness in our search family.

\subsection{Real-data replay: cropped-map and degraded-prior stress}
\label{sec:eval-realdata}
To place the tiers against real inputs, we used an environment-gated hook at the engine's
entry points to capture every NDT input: the map tile, per-align guess, and source cloud.
The capture covers \realFrames{} aligns collected after production preprocessing over
57 minutes of a real urban drive in İstanbul. We then replayed the frames offline through both
engines on identical buffers with the cost counters enabled. Because the benchmark harness could not
reproduce the production localization filter's prior quality offline, the guess track is a
frozen NDT-odometry reconstruction that uses constant-velocity extrapolation from a
global-navigation-satellite-system, or GNSS, seed. It is
\emph{representative of degraded-prior operation}, including initialization and filter
dropouts, but not of
nominal cruising, which the converged node-level baseline shows at 3 iterations per frame.

One consequence of this open-loop track must be stated up front: the offline benchmark map
is a cropped extract, and the reconstructed trajectory exits it after frame
\realOnMapSeqMax{}. Only \realOnMap{} aligns, distributed across \realSegments{} short
on-map segments within this prefix and covering ${\sim}2\%$ of the drive, perform meaningful
matching work. We call a frame \emph{on-map} when at least one of its radius searches returns
a neighbor, so $\sumnbr > 0$. On the remaining \realOffMap{}
\emph{off-map} frames, every radius search returns empty and the align is a near-no-op.
The median iteration count is \realOffMapIterMed{}, and C++ p50 is approximately
\SI{\realOffMapCppMed}{ms}. Table~\ref{tab:realdata} separates populations explicitly.
Its first panel reports $P$, max-$K$, and certificate counts over all frames. Its second
reports timing over all \realOnMap{} on-map frames as a system-level observation and over
the \realTraceOnMap{}-frame $E_{\mathrm{trace}}$ subset as the strict common-work
implementation comparison.

Accordingly, this experiment is a \emph{cropped-map, degraded-prior stress replay}, not a
representative 57-minute nominal-localization workload. It is useful for exposing numerical
branching near the available-map boundary, stressing initialization and dropout priors, and
finding port-fidelity failures. All-frame certificate counts provide capture context, but
the near-no-op off-map majority does not characterize localization fidelity; the strict
cross-language result is the \realTraceOnMap{}-frame on-map subset.

Table~\ref{tab:realdata} shows the distributions; four observations from this replay. First, the observed
inputs are consistent with the preprocessing contracts: $P \le \realPMax = 1500$ on all
\realFrames{} frames. Second, the per-point neighbor count reaches at most $\realKmaxMax$: within the
per-tile geometric bound of 27, but \emph{one above} the voxel-center heuristic of 8. Real
centroids deviate from voxel centers and pull a ninth leaf into range; the operative bound
is the 27, not the 8. Third, under degraded priors the iteration cap is \emph{routinely}
reached on real geometry, affecting \realIterCapPct\% of on-map frames and corroborating the deployment
tier's iteration axis with real data.

The timing rows report the \emph{Replay} configuration. They use
normal CFS scheduling, no pinning, one align per frame in production order, and a pinned
\SI{3.2}{GHz} reference clock on all cores. The observed-maximum frame costs
\SI{\realCppMax}{ms} in C++ and \SI{\realRustMax}{ms} in Rust. Under this degraded-prior
track, the 10\,Hz deadline is no longer a marginal worst case: \realOverrunsCpp{} C++
aligns exceed the \SI{100}{ms} budget. They represent
\realOverrunsCppOnMapPct\% of the on-map frames, whose C++ median is
\SI{\realCppMedOnMap}{ms}. Rust never exceeds the budget under this degraded-prior
track. The Rust maximum sits
$\realMarginDeploy\times$ below the deployment-tier witness and
${\sim}\realMarginEngine\times$ below the engine tier. These tier maxima are Isolated
values. For Rust, the bridge experiment justifies the comparison with
Replay-to-Isolated ratios of
$\bridgeRustInflMin$--$\bridgeRustInflMax\times$, as reported in
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-bridge}.
Two scope notes qualify this comparison. First, a production ECU's governor and boost
behavior may differ from the pinned reference clock, which was recorded rather than assumed.
Second, the replay intentionally excluded co-resident Autoware workloads to isolate
align-kernel timing and preserve cross-engine comparability. Consequently, these
measurements do not establish full-stack deadline compliance; Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion}
specifies the operational evaluation required before deployment. The counter rows are
environment-independent.

Fourth, the certificate levels differ materially. Over all frames,
$|E_{\mathrm{base}}|=\realBaseAll{}$ and
$|E_{\mathrm{trace}}|=\realTraceAll{}$. The on-map partition is
\realTraceOnMap{} trace-certified frames, \realBaseOnly{} base-only frames, and
\realIterationDivergent{} iteration-divergent frames. Thus only
\realTraceOnMapPct\% of meaningful matches support strict common-work comparison;
\realNotTraceOnMap{} frames, or \realNotTraceOnMapPct\%, do not.

Stack-level replay extends the comparison to the NDT--EKF interface, including rotation,
quality-gate, innovation, and feedback behavior. We replayed the same 57-minute cloud,
twist, and GNSS streams twice per engine in C++--Rust--Rust--C++ order. A fixed timestamp offset
preserved recorded intervals; all runs used one initial pose, a 500-m tiled map loaded within
150 m, and a 3.2 GHz governor. Unlike the serial offline comparison in
Table~\ref{tab:realdata}, the stack used the deployed four-worker configuration: four OpenMP
threads for C++ and a four-worker Rayon pool for Rust. The workers remained under CFS without
per-worker pinning, so this is a throughput observation rather than multi-core WCET evidence.
Each run consumed 34,375 clouds at 10.00 Hz. C++ NDT maxima were 45.7--46.8 ms and Rust
maxima 15.0--20.7 ms; no align exceeded 100 ms. The different parallelism and
feedback-conditioned work distribution mean that this absence of overruns does not contradict
the serial C++ overruns in Table~\ref{tab:realdata}. Raw outputs ranged
from 14,779 to 21,334 for C++ and 18,096 to 30,711 for Rust because feedback trajectories
left the differential map region. C++ therefore had per-align compute headroom for 10 Hz; missing
outputs were predominantly map-availability failures, not deadline overruns.

At the open-loop maximum-delta frame, all runs passed the NDT quality and EKF gates and the cross-language raw
XY difference was at most 3.9 mm, equal to the C++ repeat difference. Whole-drive
repeatability was insufficient for attribution: the C++/Rust repeats disagreed on
2,084/1,965 NDT quality decisions and 1,514/1,065 EKF gate decisions, while cross-language
ranges were 1,286--2,260 and 1,142--1,462. Cross-language p99 innovation differences
(1.01--1.42 m) were comparable to same-engine values (0.84--1.32 m). Feedback reached
kilometre-scale separation within one engine, and no pair recovered below 0.10 m and
0.01 rad for five frames after its maximum. The replay neither demonstrates EKF absorption
nor closed-loop equivalence. Feedback-dependent trajectories repeatedly left the available
differential map, so this result does not turn the recording into a full-map nominal
workload. Operational evaluation needs controlled initialization,
scheduling, map-edge behavior, ground truth, and co-resident workloads.

Our measurement policy links the two configurations through a mandatory bridge experiment. The
same five frozen inputs replay under both configurations: the shared counter-extremal witness, both deployment-legal

Table~\ref{tab:bridge} reports medians, maxima, and the descriptive
interference-inflation ratio, defined as production over controlled. The synthetic legs are
matched-$n$, using a pooled three-session $n{=}3000$ campaign on \emph{both} profiles, so their
max-vs-max ratios are same-$n$; the real-frame legs are $n{=}100$ on both profiles.

Two findings. For the \textbf{Rust} engine the ratio is
$\bridgeRustInflMin$--$\bridgeRustInflMax\times$ on every input: the controlled maxima

configuration is thus
\emph{associated} with a \SI{\bridgeCppTaxMinMs}{}--\SI{\bridgeCppTaxMaxMs}{ms} higher C++

immunity points at the allocator and per-query machinery; a same-core two-boot experiment

\subsection{On-target counter transfer to AArch64}
\label{sec:eval-raspi4}
\input{tables/raspi4}

To validate the cross-ISA leg of the methodology from Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-crossisa} on the
deployment-class evaluation target, we ran the same frozen fixtures through the engine on a
Raspberry Pi~4 with a Cortex-A72 under the bare-metal \texttt{no\_std} kernel. An async task
parses the embedded fixture bytes, performs one counted align each, and asserts the counters
against the host values in Table~\ref{tab:raspi4}. Every counter of every fixture transfers
\emph{exactly}, or \raspiExact{} of \raspiTotal{}.

The first hardware run matched only 7 of 8 stored expectations. Bisection identified a
stale pre-fidelity-fix host snapshot, not an ISA effect; re-baselining reconciled host,
emulator, and hardware at \raspiDeviceOk{} of \raspiTotal{}. This incident demonstrates
the certificate sensitivity and the need to commit-pin expected counters; the FMA, QEMU,
and subnormal checks are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:crossisa-debug}.

The timing columns of Table~\ref{tab:raspi4} report the \emph{Bare-metal} series, an amendment
to the measurement policy for OS-less targets. For each fixture, the series contains
\raspiTimingSamples{} warm and \raspiTimingCold{} cold samples on the warm map and is
bracketed by a fixed-work
calibration spin, the on-target analog of the host campaign's throttle guard and the
relevant hazard here, since the Pi~4 throttles under sustained load. Over the
${\sim}55$-minute run the calibration drifted at most \raspiCalibDriftPct\%: no thermal
throttling. The distributions are very tight: worst warm max/p50 across all fixtures is
${+}\raspiSpreadWorstPct\%$. The cold series uses an 8\,MiB eviction sweep before every
sample and sits at most ${+}\raspiColdDeltaWorstPct\%$ above the warm maximum, tighter than
the Isolated host series, as expected with no OS, one active core, and a
firmware-fixed clock. This is the platform class where the factorization of
Sec.~\ref{sec:threats} is closest to its intended use: the work bound transfers exactly
through the counters, and the per-unit cost has sub-percent spread and is nearly deterministic. Thus,
$\text{work bound} \times \text{unit-cost bound}$ approaches an analytical WCET. In
absolute terms the serial configuration is not schedulable at 10\,Hz on this target: the
engine-tier worst costs \SI{\raspiSearchWarmMaxS}{s}, or $\raspiHostFactor\times$ the
x86 host's Isolated maximum. Even the deployment-tier witness \emph{legal-worst} costs
$\raspiLegalWorstBudgetX\times$ the \SI{100}{ms} budget; Table~\ref{tab:raspi4} gives
the per-fixture values. The Pi~4 is the evaluation target rather than the production ECU,
so we read this as a requirement on the production platform: roughly
$\raspiLegalWorstBudgetX\times$ the A72's per-unit-work speed is needed on the deployment
witness alone, and the measured x86 desktop host, at
$\raspiHostFactorMin$--$\raspiHostFactorMax\times$ the A72 per unit work on these fixtures,
still misses that witness by $\legalWorstBudgetXRust\times$, as shown in
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-twotier}. The affine $T_{\mathrm{obs,max}}(P)$ envelope of
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-psweep} lets any candidate platform be audited with a single on-target
$P$-sweep. The remaining levers are $P$ reduction, SIMD, algorithmic change, and
parallelization. Section~\ref{sec:eval-parallel} measures approximately
\parRustSpeedupFour$\times$ at four host cores.

\subsection{Extreme-value analysis: what the diagnostics permit}
\label{sec:eval-evt}
For each tail fixture we fitted per-session POT/GPD models to $3{\times}1000$ Isolated
samples and applied the full diagnostic gate. Lag-1--10 autocorrelation reaches
\evtAcfWorst{} rather than the ${\sim}0.03$ expected under independence; consequently the
bootstrap resamples dependent exceedances and its $q_{10^{-9}}$ intervals are invalid. We
therefore report no extrapolated exceedance probabilities and retain only the empirical
maxima in Table~\ref{tab:tails}. Table~\ref{tab:evt} in Appendix~\ref{app:evt} gives the
fixture-level ACF and POT/GPD diagnostics and illustrates why plausible-looking fits do
not override failed assumptions.
