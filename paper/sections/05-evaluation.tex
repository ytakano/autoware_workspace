\section{Evaluation}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}
All engine-tier timing follows \textbf{Profile B (controlled engine)} of our measurement
policy. Host: \envCpu{}, \envKernel{}; the benchmark core is isolated
(\texttt{isolcpus}/\texttt{nohz\_full}, IRQ affinities moved, 3 immovable remaining), its
SMT sibling offline, and its frequency \emph{pinned} at \SI{3.2}{GHz} under the
\envGovernor{} governor; \envCppCompiler{} (C++, \texttt{-O2}); \envRustCompiler{}
(release). \envSessions{} sessions (\envDate, one across a reboot cycle) with randomized
fixture and engine order; per fixture and engine, \envItersWarmPerSession{} warm
${+}$ \envItersTailPerSession{} tail samples per session after \envWarmup{} warmups, pooled
across the sessions (warm series; the cold and co-runner series appear in
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-interference}); \texttt{steady\_clock}, align loop only (map and kd-tree
built once per engine per fixture). A fixed-work calibration guard aborts any series whose
sustained speed shifts by more than 10\%; it fired twice on transient firmware-level
clamps, which were excluded by re-running the affected series. Engines were measured at
autoware\_core commit \texttt{\envCommit}; per-run manifests (environment, compiler flags,
commits, fixture and binary hashes) are embedded in the released data files.

\subsection{Search effectiveness}
\label{sec:eval-search}
The hill-climb saturates the analytic kernel-evaluation maximum exactly:
$\sumnbr = P \cdot \maxnn \cdot (N_{\mathrm{iter}}{+}1) = 2000 \cdot 64 \cdot 31 =
\num{3968000}$, certifying that the hand-built \emph{dense-neighbors} construction is
kernel-count-optimal. It then grows the remaining free term \kdnodes{} by a further
\searchKdGrowthPct\% (\oneoffKdGenZeroM\,M $\rightarrow$ \searchZeroKdM\,M, map extent 6
$\rightarrow$ 9 blocks; the generation-0 value is from the recorded search log, the final
value from the frozen fixture's counters).
The frozen union-worst fixture (\emph{search-00}; ``union-worst'' throughout means worst in
the \emph{counter} order, i.e.\ counter-extremal) dominates every other frozen fixture on
every engine and percentile (Table~\ref{tab:tails}); the frontier ablation below sharpens
the scope of that statement. Table~\ref{tab:counters} lists the frozen set.

\emph{Ablations} (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). The design supports three claims. First, the

\emph{Counter-Pareto frontier.} A lexicographic order can in principle mis-rank the
\emph{time}-worst input: a candidate trading kernel evaluations for heavier traversal could

\subsection{Engine-tier frame times: C++ vs.\ Rust}
\label{sec:eval-tails}
The equal-work certificate holds on every fixture: identical \texttt{iteration\_num} per
engine pair, final poses agreeing to at most \certMaxTransDelta\,\si{m} of translation, and
both convergence scores bit-identical on \certScoreExact{} of \certFixtures{} fixtures (the
largest score delta, \certMaxScoreDelta{} on \emph{subnormal}, is one \texttt{f32} ULP).
The comparison is therefore algorithm-fair. The Rust port's observed maximum is at or below
the C++ engine's on every fixture (Table~\ref{tab:tails}; ratios
\tailsRatioMin--\tailsRatioMax; union worst: \SI{\tailsSearchMaxCpp}{ms} $\rightarrow$
\SI{\tailsSearchMaxRust}{ms}).

The \emph{subnormal} fixture's outlier ratio ($\subnormalMedianRatio\times$ at p50) required
a controlled check. Re-generating the same fixture with the source shell pulled from
\SI{1.99}{m} to \SI{1.69}{m}, which moves every kernel $\exp$ result out of the subnormal
band with all else equal, left the C++ time \emph{unchanged}
(\SI{\oneoffShellSubCpp}{ms} vs.\ \SI{\oneoffShellCtrlCpp}{ms} p50;
Rust \oneoffShellSubRust{} vs.\ \oneoffShellCtrlRust; measured in the pre-protocol
environment, so the absolute values are era-specific but the null A/B result is not).
Subnormal operands are therefore \emph{not} the mechanism on this microarchitecture; a
microbenchmark confirms that subnormal-result \texttt{exp} costs only
$\oneoffExpSubGlibc\times$/$\oneoffExpSubLibm\times$ per call for glibc/\texttt{libm},
negligible at this fixture's $\bar{K}=\subnormalKbar$.

The real driver is the \emph{per-point constant overhead} exposed by a neighbor-sparse
shape: with almost no kernel work, C++ spends
${\approx}\subnormalPtPassCppUs$\,\si{\micro\second} per point per pass against Rust's
$\subnormalPtPassRustUs$, an ${\approx}\ptPassGapUs$\,\si{\micro\second} gap that reappears
as the same constant on \emph{cache-hostile} ($\bar{K}=\cacheHostileKbar$). Decomposing it:
the measured allocator fast path (\SI{\oneoffMallocPairNs}{ns} per malloc/free pair
$\times$ ${\approx}\subnormalAllocPairs$ pairs) accounts for only
${\sim}\subnormalAllocSharePct\%$; the remainder is the per-query search machinery around
pcl/FLANN (call indirection and result-container handling) that the Rust engine's direct,
allocation-free kd query does not pay. This sharpens the allocation story of
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-alloc}: heap churn is cheap \emph{on average}, and its significance is
the unbounded latency tail, while the mean-cost gap on sparse inputs is search-machinery
overhead. (No hardware performance counters are available in the measurement container, so
the final split is arithmetic over microbenchmarks rather than counter-attributed;
Sec.~\ref{sec:threats}.)

\subsection{Per-input trace certificate}
\label{sec:eval-tracecert}
\input{tables/tracecert}

The trace certificate of Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer} was executed on every frozen fixture
and on all \realFrames{} captured real frames: the traced analysis build of the C++ engine
(an instrumented copy of the engine sources plus a counting FLANN distance functor; the
shipped sources remain byte-identical) emits a per-pass trace that the replay compares leg
by leg against the Rust engine's mirrored trace. Every quantity is a deterministic function
of the input, so the runs need no controlled environment, and the traced binaries are never
used for timing.

Table~\ref{tab:tracecert} shows the fixture leg. The \emph{structural} legs (pass count,
per-pass point and neighbor counts, and each point's neighbor-leaf set) transfer
\emph{exactly} on all \traceFixtures{} inputs, the line-search loop is never entered, and
the measured C++ pass count equals $N_{\mathrm{iter}}{+}1$ on every input: the equal-work
transfer previously certified indirectly (iteration/pose/score plus the allocation leg of
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-alloc}) is now measured directly on the C++ side, per input. The
\emph{numeric} legs sharpen the fidelity picture: the per-pass score agrees to
\traceUlpConvMax{} f64 ULP or better on the well-conditioned fixtures (bit-identical on
\emph{cache-hostile}) but drifts to $\traceUlpWorstTex$ ULP on knife-edge geometries. The
mechanism is structural, not a defect: pcl/FLANN returns each point's neighbors sorted by
distance while the port's kd walk returns them in visit order, so the two engines
accumulate identical per-point neighbor sets in different orders; f64 bit-equality of the
sums is unattainable by design, and equivalence is correctly claimed at the \emph{work}
level.

The real-data leg turns the necessary-vs-sufficient distinction into measurement. The
traced replay reproduces the frozen capture frame-exactly (identical per-frame iteration
counts and match verdicts on all \realFrames{} frames). Of the \traceRealCertOnMap{} on-map
frames that pass the base (equal-iteration) certificate, \traceRealWorkCertOnMap{} also
pass the full trace certificate, while \traceRealWorkGapFrames{} perform
\emph{equal-iteration but unequal work}: their per-point neighbor sets differ (median
$|\Delta\sumnbr| = \traceRealWorkGapNbrMed$, maximum \traceRealWorkGapNbrMax, i.e.\ at most
\traceRealWorkGapRelMaxPct\% of the frame's kernel evaluations) --- the same knife-edge
boundary mechanism as the iteration divergences, caught below the iteration threshold.
Equal iteration count is therefore measurably \emph{not} sufficient for equal work, and the
strict work-certified set on this drive is $E_{\mathrm{trace}} = \traceRealETrace$ of
\realFrames{} frames (all off-map frames certify exactly). The \realDivergences{}
iteration-divergent frames all fork late (first divergent pass
\traceRealDivFirstMin--\traceRealDivFirstMax, median \traceRealDivFirstMed) and then take
different convergence paths (iteration counts up to $\pm\traceRealDivIterDeltaMax$ apart,
final poses up to \SI{\traceRealDivTransDeltaMax}{m} apart). Every fixture-level comparison
in this paper carries the full trace certificate; real-data work statements are scoped to
$E_{\mathrm{trace}}$.

\subsection{Heap allocations per align}
\label{sec:eval-alloc}
The interposer counts decompose as ${\approx}\allocPerPtPass$ allocations per point per
derivative pass on every fixture (e.g.\ $\allocWorstPerAlign{} / (31 \cdot 2000) =
\allocPerPtPassExact$), locating the source in the per-point inner loop: a fresh neighbor
vector constructed inside the parallel loop body, two fresh index/distance vectors per
radius-search call, and per-query result containers inside pcl/FLANN (${\approx}8$/point).
These ride the allocator's thread-cache fast path, invisible on average, but each is a
potential lock, page fault, or fragmentation stall inside the innermost loop. The Rust
engine hoists all three allocation sites into a pre-reserved workspace and measures zero in
the same harness, consistent with its counting-allocator-verified allocation freedom
(Table~\ref{tab:alloc}).

Beyond the hazard reading, these counts double as an \emph{independent work certificate for
the C++ side} (Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer}): the numerator comes from the untouched
production binary via \texttt{LD\_PRELOAD}, the denominator ($P \cdot N_{\mathrm{pass}}$)
from the Rust counters, and the ratio is \allocCertMin--\allocCertMax{} on every fixture
whose radius queries return neighbors. This is a \allocCertSpreadPct\% spread across
geometries whose cost differs by two orders of magnitude, fine enough that a single extra
C++ derivative pass (a ${\sim}3\%$ shift at $N_{\mathrm{pass}} = 31$) could not hide. The
one deviation, \emph{subnormal} at \allocCertSparse{}, is itself structural: its
mostly-empty radius searches skip the per-result containers.

\subsection{Cache and interference sensitivity}
\label{sec:eval-interference}
\input{tables/interference}

Two further Profile-B series probe how fragile the warm-series numbers are
(Table~\ref{tab:interference}). The \emph{cold} series (between-sample software cache
eviction) shifts medians by at most \coldWorstPct\% across the whole fixture set: the
worst-case-shaped inputs are already cache-hostile, so eviction adds little. The
\emph{co-runner} series (one hardware resource stressed at a time from an L3-sharing core)
splits cleanly along the two-tier structure. On \emph{search-00}, whose cost is
kernel-evaluation-bound and cache-resident, no co-runner moves the maximum by more than
${\sim}\coWorstSearch\%$; on \emph{legal-worst}, whose cost is kd-traversal/memory-bound
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-twotier}), the memory-bandwidth co-runner inflates the observed maximum
by up to $+\coWorstLegal\%$ (Rust) while the LLC and FP co-runners remain null. The
deployment tier, not the engine-tier worst, is thus the interference-sensitive one, which
is what its kd-dominated cost structure predicts.

\subsection{Unit-cost regression}
\label{sec:eval-regression}
We estimate the unit costs of the counter-form bound (Sec.~\ref{sec:decomposition}) by
regressing median latency on the counters over the fixtures pooled with the $P$-sweep
points ($n = \regN$ per engine), fitting two nested models per engine: the two-term model
$T \approx a\cdot\sumnbr + b\cdot\mathrm{kd} + c$ and the four-term model that adds the
per-point fixed cost $c_{\mathrm{pt}} \cdot N_{\mathrm{pts}}$ (Table~\ref{tab:regression}).
The trace certificate (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-tracecert}) supplies the C++ engine's own
traversal counter, so the kd regressor is engine-own on both sides and $b$ is a physical
per-operation cost for both engines. The per-point axis remains engine-asymmetric. For C++
it is real but not separately identified: adding it collapses the worst leave-one-out miss
from \regLooMaxCppTwoTerm\% to \regLooMaxCppFourTerm\% (median \regLooMedCpp\%), yet the
coefficient's CI covers zero ([\regPtUsCppLo,\,\regPtUsCppHi]\,\si{\micro\second}) because
FLANN's per-query work is itself nearly proportional to the point count --- the per-point
machinery cost is shared between $c_{\mathrm{pt}}$ and $b$, with the point estimate
(\SI{\regPtUsCpp}{\micro\second}) consistent with the
${\approx}\subnormalPtPassCppUs$\,\si{\micro\second} per-point overhead measured
independently in Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-tails}. For Rust the term is degenerate outright: the
fitted $c_{\mathrm{pt}}$ is negative (\SI{\regPtUsRustFour}{\micro\second}, CI
[\regPtUsRustFourLo,\,\regPtUsRustFourHi]) and the worst leave-one-out miss \emph{grows}
from \regLooMaxRustTwoTerm\% to \regLooMaxRustFourTerm\%, because the port's true per-point
cost (${\approx}\subnormalPtPassRustUs$\,\si{\micro\second}, measured directly in
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-tails}) sits below this design's resolution. We therefore adopt the
four-term model for C++ (the only model that covers the neighbor-sparse regime) and the
two-term model for Rust ($\star$ in Table~\ref{tab:regression}): the axis the two-term
model was missing is a \emph{C++} phenomenon (the allocator and per-query search machinery
of Secs.~\ref{sec:eval-tails}--\ref{sec:eval-alloc}), and its absence from the Rust fit is
consistent with the port removing exactly that machinery.

Three diagnostics scope the fits. First, the regressors are nearly collinear over this
design (worst pairwise correlation \regCorr; on the $P$-sweep subset $N_{\mathrm{pts}}$
and \sumnbr{} are exactly proportional), so the individual coefficients share variance and
carry the wide bootstrap CIs shown in the table; the combined prediction is far better
identified than any single coefficient. Second, the adopted models' remaining worst
leave-one-out misses are \regLooWorstCpp{} (\regLooMaxCpp\%, C++) and \regLooWorstRust{}
(\regLooMaxRust\%, Rust): the Rust two-term model still cannot see the (small) per-point
constant on the neighbor-sparse fixtures. Third, the negative Rust intercept is a fitting
artifact whose bootstrap CI covers zero.

What survives these diagnostics: within the adopted models, prediction is accurate on the
dense-neighbor and $P$-sweep regime that dominates the design (median leave-one-out error
\regLooMedCpp\%/\regLooMedRust\%), and the C++ four-term fit now also covers the
neighbor-sparse regime. Coefficient-level \emph{cross-engine} comparisons remain
indicative only: the adopted models differ in form, and even under a common two-term model
the kernel-cost CIs overlap. The apparent kernel-cost gap (\SI{\regKernelNsCpp}{ns} vs.\
\SI{\regKernelNsRust}{ns} under the adopted models) is consistent with the overhead
structure of Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-tails} (each C++ evaluation reaches its leaf through a
pointer indirection, where the Rust engine reads a flat leaf array), though without
hardware counters we present this attribution as arithmetic-plausible rather than
counter-proven. The kd coefficients are engine-own per-operation costs
(\SI{\regKdNsCpp}{ns} per FLANN evaluation vs.\ \SI{\regKdNsRust}{ns} per node examined)
and are not mutually comparable across engines. The \emph{resolved}
engine comparison is the direct per-fixture one (Table~\ref{tab:tails}: max-latency ratios
\tailsRatioMin--\tailsRatioMax, stable to $\pm\sessRatioSpreadWorst$ across sessions),
which needs no decomposition. The kd term is second-order on the engine-tier worst set,
though it dominates the deployment tier (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-twotier}).

\subsection{Scaling with the input size $P$}
\label{sec:eval-psweep}
In the decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp}, $P$ is the only multiplicative freedom: it scales the
per-iteration sum while leaving $N_{\mathrm{iter}}$, $K$, and $T_{\mathrm{solve}}$ untouched,
predicting an \emph{affine} observed envelope $T_{\mathrm{obs,max}}(P) =
\mathrm{slope}\cdot P + \mathrm{const}$ (a fit to observed maxima, not a hard bound).
We validate this with a controlled sweep: the union-worst geometry regenerated at $P \in
[\psweepPmin{}, \psweepPmax{}]$ (six points, $\times 2$ steps), where the counters certify
that per-point work is exactly invariant ($N_{\mathrm{iter}} = 30$ and $\sumnbr = P \cdot
64 \cdot 31$ at every $P$), so time may vary only through $P$ itself.
Table~\ref{tab:psweep} and Fig.~\ref{fig:psweep} show the result: both engines are affine in
$P$ with $R^2 = \psweepRsqCpp{}$ (C++) and $\psweepRsqRust{}$ (Rust) on the \emph{max} series;
no cache knee is visible over the tested range. The fitted worst-case cost is
\SI{\psweepSlopeCpp}{\micro\second} (C++) vs.\ \SI{\psweepSlopeRust}{\micro\second} (Rust)
per source point, consistent with the unit costs of
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-regression} (${\approx}64$ kernel evaluations $\times$ 31 passes per
point, plus the per-point and traversal terms).

The affine envelope makes the result \emph{parametric in the caller's contract}: inverting it
against the \SI{100}{ms} (10\,Hz) budget gives a maximum admissible downsampled-scan size of
$P \approx \psweepPmaxRust{}$ points for the Rust engine (\psweepPmaxCpp{} for C++)
\emph{under the union-worst geometry on this host}, a number the localization node's
voxel-filter configuration can be audited against directly. (Host-relative; on deployment
hardware the same protocol yields the slope that matters there.)

\subsection{Two-tier bound: engine tier vs.\ deployment tier}
\label{sec:eval-twotier}
The adversarial fixtures bound the engine at its API over the bounded-neighbor domain $D$
(the bounded-neighbor engine tier; Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer}): the right contract for
the \texttt{no\_std} kernel deployment, where inputs cross a trust boundary unvalidated. When the production
preprocessing contracts hold (Sec.~\ref{sec:impl-legal}), the reachable input space
shrinks, and with it the observed worst case: on \emph{legal-worst}, the hardest
preprocessing-reachable input we constructed, the observed maximum drops to
\SI{\legalWorstMaxRust}{ms} (Rust) / \SI{\legalWorstMaxCpp}{ms} (C++), a measured factor
of \legalWorstRatioRust$\times$ / \legalWorstRatioCpp$\times$ below the engine-tier union
worst (Table~\ref{tab:tails}).

Two caveats scope this factor. It is a \emph{fixture-to-fixture measurement}, not a proven
tightening: \emph{legal-worst} realizes the voxel-center heuristic $K = 8$ per point, while
the tier's provable ceiling is $K \le 27$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:implementation}); the tightening
the contracts alone guarantee on the kernel term is the parametric $64/27 \approx
2.4\times$, and a legal input between our witness and that ceiling may exist. And it is
conditional on the contracts actually holding: the engine does not check them
(Table~\ref{tab:preconditions}).

The mechanism is the $K$ ceiling: disjoint voxel-aligned map tiles cap leaf multiplicity at
1, capping $K$ at 27 (realized at $\bar{K} = \legalWorstKbar$ by this witness) instead of
64, and the production random-downsample cap fixes $P \le 1500$. Note the shift in the cost
structure: kernel evaluations drop $\twoTierKernelDrop\times$ ($\sumnbr$:
$\searchZeroSumNbrM\,\mathrm{M}$ $\rightarrow \legalWorstSumNbrK\,\mathrm{k}$) but time
drops less, because kd-tree traversal, unaffected by the $K$ ceiling, becomes the dominant
term of the deployment tier. Read constructively, the pair quantifies what \emph{enforcing
input contracts at the component boundary} buys: validating tile disjointness and
scan-downsample invariants before the align narrows the observed worst case by a measured
$\legalWorstRatioRust\times$ (provably $\ge 2.4\times$ on the kernel term) without touching
the kernel itself. The iteration axis of the tier is not an artifact of the $\epsilon$ pin:
the production-parameterized legal search (Sec.~\ref{sec:impl-legal}) reaches the full
30-iteration cap on \emph{legal-osc} (\SI{\legalOscMaxRust}{ms} Rust /
\SI{\legalOscMaxCpp}{ms} C++, $\bar{K} = \legalOscKbar$), below \emph{legal-worst}, which
therefore remains the tier's hardest measured witness.

\subsection{Closing the residual gap with parallelism}
\label{sec:eval-parallel}
\input{tables/parallel}

Even under the deployment contracts the serial Rust engine misses the \SI{100}{ms} budget on
the tier's witness (\emph{legal-worst}, \SI{\parRustLegalSerialMs}{ms}). Both engines,
however, expose a thread count: the C++ engine through OpenMP, and the Rust port through a
rayon-backed derivative reduction that is \emph{order-preserving and bit-identical to the
serial backend} (the per-point contributions are folded in point index order regardless of
worker count), so parallelism changes only wall time, never the result. We measure it as a
throughput lever, not as an extension of the WCET argument: the serial engine remains the
deterministic baseline of this paper, and a multi-core \emph{WCET} analysis (worker
interference, scheduling) is out of scope.

We ran both engines on $k \in \{1,2,4\}$ isolated physical cores, one worker pinned per core
(the C++ threads via \texttt{GOMP\_CPU\_AFFINITY}, the rayon workers via a pool that pins
worker $i$ to the $i$-th core of the process's CPU set --- necessary because
\texttt{isolcpus} disables the kernel's automatic load balancing, so unpinned workers would
share one core). Table~\ref{tab:parallel} reports the result: both engines scale nearly
linearly (Rust ${\approx}\parRustSpeedupFour\times$, C++ ${\approx}\parCppSpeedupFour\times$
at $k{=}4$), and the Rust engine's iteration counts are identical across $k$ (the
bit-identical property, now measured, not just asserted). The deployment-tier residual
closes at $k = \parRustLegalFitK$: the Rust engine brings \emph{legal-worst} to
\SI{\parRustLegalFitMaxMs}{ms}, inside the \SI{100}{ms} budget. Read against the two tiers,
the \SI{100}{ms} deadline on this host is met by the composition of enforcing the input
contracts (a measured $\legalWorstRatioRust\times$; Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-twotier}) and two
pinned cores. These are host observations at the \SI{3.2}{GHz} reference clock; the same
$k$-scaling protocol, run on the deployment target, gives the core count that platform
requires (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-raspi4}).

\subsection{Real-data replay: contract validation and degraded-prior behavior}
\label{sec:eval-realdata}
To place the tiers against real inputs, we captured every NDT input (map tile, per-align
guess and source cloud) during a replay of a real urban drive (İstanbul; \realFrames{} aligns
over 57 minutes through the production preprocessing) via an environment-gated hook at the
engine's entry points, then replayed the captured frames offline through both engines on
identical buffers with the cost counters enabled. Because the benchmark harness could not
reproduce the production localization filter's prior quality offline, the guess track is a
frozen NDT-odometry reconstruction (constant-velocity extrapolation from a
global-navigation-satellite-system (GNSS) seed):
\emph{representative of degraded-prior operation} (initialization, filter dropouts), not of
nominal cruising, which the converged node-level baseline shows at 3 iterations per frame.

One consequence of this open-loop track must be stated up front: the offline benchmark map
is a cropped extract, and the reconstructed trajectory exits it after frame
\realOnMapSeqMax{}, so only \realOnMap{} aligns, a prefix window covering ${\sim}2\%$ of the
drive, perform meaningful matching work. We call a frame \emph{on-map} when at least one of
its radius searches returns a neighbor ($\sumnbr > 0$); on the remaining \realOffMap{}
\emph{off-map} frames every radius search returns empty and the align is a near-no-op
(iteration count p50 $= \realOffMapIterMed$, C++ p50
${\approx}\SI{\realOffMapCppMed}{ms}$). The per-frame envelope rows of
Table~\ref{tab:realdata} therefore use the on-map subset, while the $P$ and max-$K$
contract checks, which do not depend on map coverage, use all frames. The align-time rows
are additionally reported over the \emph{certified} subset (the \realOnMapMatch{} on-map
frames whose base equal-iteration certificate passed): the all-on-map rows are the
system-level envelope, the certified rows the equal-work implementation comparison. The
stricter trace certificate subdivides the certified frames further
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-tracecert}).

Table~\ref{tab:realdata} shows the distributions; four observations. First, the observed
inputs are consistent with the preprocessing contracts: $P \le \realPMax = 1500$ on all
\realFrames{} frames. Second, the per-point neighbor count reaches at most $\realKmaxMax$: within the
per-tile geometric bound of 27, but \emph{one above} the voxel-center heuristic of 8. Real
centroids deviate from voxel centers and pull a ninth leaf into range; the operative bound
is the 27, not the 8. Third, under degraded priors the iteration cap is \emph{routinely}
reached on real geometry (\realIterCapPct\% of on-map frames), corroborating the deployment
tier's iteration axis with real data.

The timing rows are \textbf{Profile-A} results under the measurement policy (normal CFS
scheduling, no pinning, one align per frame in production order, all cores at the pinned
\SI{3.2}{GHz} reference clock): the real worst frame costs \SI{\realCppMax}{ms} (C++) vs.\
\SI{\realRustMax}{ms} (Rust). At the 10\,Hz deadline this is no longer a marginal worst
case: \realOverrunsCpp{} C++ aligns (\realOverrunsCppOnMapPct\% of the on-map frames, with
an on-map C++ median of \SI{\realCppMedOnMap}{ms}) exceed the \SI{100}{ms} budget under
this degraded-prior track, while Rust never does. The Rust maximum sits
$\realMarginDeploy\times$ below the deployment-tier witness and

Locating that residual sharpens its reading. The \realOnMap{} on-map frames form

the knife-edge fork mechanism of Sec.~\ref{sec:threats} (a late derivative pass rounds
differently and the engines then take different convergence paths), not of a systematic

A system-level reading of these pose deltas, offered as interpretation rather than
measurement: in production only one engine runs, so the deltas quantify
\emph{substitutability}, not an error either system observes. On the work-certified frames
the engines agree to a median of \traceRealTransDeltaMed\,\si{m}, orders of magnitude below
the NDT measurement's own noise; the \SI{\traceRealTransDeltaMax}{m}--%
\SI{\traceRealDivTransDeltaMax}{m} tails arise precisely on the ill-conditioned frames
(oscillating, degraded-prior, map-edge) where the measurement is low-quality for
\emph{either} engine, the forks carry no systematic sign, and these are the frames the
production pipeline is built to attenuate --- the transform-probability/NVTL publish gates,
the oscillation diagnostic (Sec.~\ref{sec:impl-legal}), the estimated pose covariance, and
the downstream filter's innovation gating. Whether that attenuation fully absorbs an engine
swap is a \emph{closed-loop} question that our open-loop replay cannot answer, so we state
it as a deployment precaution rather than deferring it: any engine substitution should be
accompanied by closed-loop validation, on-vehicle or in simulation, that monitors the
quality-gate hit rate and the downstream filter's innovation statistics on exactly the
ill-conditioned segments where the implementations can part ways (initialization,
feature-poor geometry, map edges).

\subsection{Bridge experiment: controlled vs.\ production profiles}
\label{sec:eval-bridge}
The two profiles are linked by a mandatory bridge experiment (our measurement policy): the
same five frozen inputs (the union-worst, both deployment-legal witnesses, and the
slowest and median-cost real frames, frozen from the drive capture) replayed under both
profiles at the same \SI{3.2}{GHz} reference clock, same harness, differing only in the
environment. Table~\ref{tab:bridge} reports medians, maxima, and the descriptive
interference-inflation ratio (production over controlled). One design caveat: the
synthetic legs compare maxima over unequal sample counts (A: $n{=}100$ per cell; B: pooled
$n{=}3000$), which biases the B maxima upward; the medians are robust to $n$ and agree
with the max-based reading.

\input{tables/bridge}

Two findings. For the \textbf{Rust} engine the ratio is
$\bridgeRustInflMin$--$\bridgeRustInflMax\times$ on every input: the controlled maxima
transfer to the production CFS configuration essentially unchanged (on an otherwise-idle
host; co-resident load is a separate axis, probed in Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-interference}).

For the \textbf{C++} engine the ratio is \emph{uniformly below one}
($\bridgeCppInflMin$--$\bridgeCppInflMax\times$): the controlled environment is not
engine-neutral. A same-boot control narrows the cause. Pinned to the isolated core the C++
align costs \SI{194.9}{ms} (\emph{legal-osc}) against \SI{165.8}{ms} on a non-isolated core
of the same boot, same pin, while Rust, in the same binary, is unchanged. Profile B is thus
\emph{associated} with a \SI{\bridgeCppTaxMinMs}{}--\SI{\bridgeCppTaxMaxMs}{ms} higher C++
per-align constant on the measured inputs; because this control varies the physical core
and the per-core isolation flags
(\texttt{isolcpus}/\texttt{nohz\_full}/\texttt{rcu\_nocbs}) together, the responsible
mechanism remains unresolved (the zero-allocation engine's immunity points at the
allocator/per-query machinery; a same-core two-boot experiment would settle it). Frequency,
SMT-sibling state, transparent hugepages, and fault counts were ruled out by direct A/B
tests; a fixed-work calibration spin runs marginally \emph{faster} on the isolated core.
Consequently the Profile-B C++ maxima are \emph{conservative} for this production
configuration, and every cross-engine conclusion of Table~\ref{tab:tails} survives under
Profile A, although the relative gap narrows (legal-worst max ratio
\bridgeGapBLegalWorst{} under B vs.\ \bridgeGapALegalWorst{} under A). The policy's caution
that scheduler and interference costs must not be assumed additive or constant is
confirmed: for one engine the ``interference inflation'' is not even ${\ge}1$.

\subsection{On-target counter transfer (AArch64)}
\label{sec:eval-raspi4}
\input{tables/raspi4}

To validate the cross-ISA leg of the methodology (Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-crossisa}) on the
deployment-class evaluation target, we ran the same frozen fixtures through the engine on a
Raspberry Pi~4 (Cortex-A72) under the bare-metal \texttt{no\_std} kernel: an async task
parses the embedded fixture bytes, performs one counted align each, and asserts the counters
against the host values (Table~\ref{tab:raspi4}). The result: every counter of every fixture
transfers \emph{exactly} (\raspiExact{} of \raspiTotal).

The result was reached via a detour that stress-tested the certificate. The \emph{first}
hardware run reported 7 of 8 against the expectation table then embedded in the kernel app
(that log is preserved in the repository history): on \emph{search-01} the measured work
differed by $\sumnbr\,{+}\raspiNbrDelta$ / $\kdnodes\,{-}\raspiKdDelta$, deterministically,
and bit-identically under QEMU-TCG emulation and on the Cortex-A72 silicon. After the
ISA-level mechanisms were excluded (no fused-multiply-add instructions in the kernel image;
the FPU at IEEE defaults, with the \emph{subnormal} fixture transferring exactly), commit
bisection located the true cause \emph{off-target}: the embedded expectations had been
transcribed from a host snapshot that predated one of the two port-fidelity fixes of
Sec.~\ref{sec:threats} (the pcl f32 transform-association mirror), which shifts this
knife-edge fixture's work by \raspiNbrDelta{} evaluations in \raspiMismatchNbrM\,M.
Re-baselining against the fixed engine reconciles host, emulator, and hardware exactly: the
re-captured hardware run (Table~\ref{tab:raspi4}) reports \raspiDeviceOk{} of \raspiTotal{}
on-device, with counters bit-identical between the two hardware runs. No measurable ISA
dependence remains, and the flag demonstrated the certificate's parts-per-million
sensitivity: the counters double as a behavioral fingerprint of the engine \emph{version},
which is why baselines must be pinned to engine commits (the artifact-manifest discipline
of Sec.~\ref{sec:threats}).

The timing columns of Table~\ref{tab:raspi4} are a \textbf{Profile-C} series (an amendment
to the measurement policy for OS-less targets): per fixture, \raspiTimingSamples{} warm and
\raspiTimingCold{} cold samples on the warm map, the series bracketed by a fixed-work
calibration spin, the on-target analog of the host campaign's throttle guard and the
relevant hazard here, since the Pi~4 throttles under sustained load. Over the
${\sim}55$-minute run the calibration drifted at most \raspiCalibDriftPct\%: no thermal
throttling. The distributions are very tight: worst warm max/p50 across all fixtures is
${+}\raspiSpreadWorstPct\%$, and the cold series (an 8\,MiB eviction sweep before every
sample) sits at most ${+}\raspiColdDeltaWorstPct\%$ above the warm maximum, tighter than
the isolated-host Profile-B series, as expected with no OS, one active core, and a
firmware-fixed clock. This is the platform class where the factorization of
Sec.~\ref{sec:threats} pays off: the work bound transfers exactly (the counters), the
per-unit cost is nearly deterministic (sub-percent spread), so
$\text{work bound} \times \text{unit-cost bound}$ approaches an analytical WCET. In
absolute terms the serial configuration is not schedulable at 10\,Hz on this target: the
engine-tier worst costs \SI{\raspiSearchWarmMaxS}{s} ($\raspiHostFactor\times$ the x86
host's Profile-B maximum), and even the deployment-tier witness \emph{legal-worst} costs
$\raspiLegalWorstBudgetX\times$ the \SI{100}{ms} budget (per-fixture values in
Table~\ref{tab:raspi4}). The Pi~4 is the evaluation target rather than the production ECU,
so we read this as a requirement on the production platform: roughly
$\raspiLegalWorstBudgetX\times$ the A72's per-unit-work speed is needed on the deployment
witness alone, and the measured x86 desktop host, at
$\raspiHostFactorMin$--$\raspiHostFactorMax\times$ the A72 per unit work on these fixtures,
still misses that witness by $\legalWorstBudgetXRust\times$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-twotier}). The affine $T_{\mathrm{obs,max}}(P)$ envelope of
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-psweep} lets any candidate platform be audited with a single on-target
$P$-sweep; the remaining levers are $P$ reduction, parallelization (measured on the host in
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-parallel}: ${\approx}\parRustSpeedupFour\times$ at four cores), SIMD, and
algorithmic change.

\subsection{Extreme-value analysis: what the diagnostics permit}
\label{sec:eval-evt}
\input{tables/evt}

With $3{\times}1000$ Profile-B samples per tail-claim fixture, we ran the
measurement-based-\pwcet{} analysis in full: peaks-over-threshold fits of a generalized
Pareto distribution by maximum likelihood, per session (pooling would mix the documented
cross-session level shifts), with a threshold sweep, \evtBoot{} bootstrap draws for CIs,
and the policy's diagnostic checklist. \emph{The diagnostics gate the conclusion}
(Table~\ref{tab:evt}). The sample series carry strong serial dependence (lag-1..10
autocorrelation magnitudes up to \evtAcfWorst{}, where an i.i.d.\ series of this length
would show ${\sim}0.03$), because on an isolated, frequency-pinned core the residual
variability is a slowly wandering band of ${\sim}0.1$--$0.5\%$, not independent noise. The
violated independence assumption surfaces exactly where theory predicts: the bootstrapped
$10^{-9}$ per-align quantiles have upper CI ends diverging to as much as
\SI{\evtCiExplodeS}{s} on the tightest fixtures. Following our measurement policy (which
forbids mechanical EVT on data that fails its assumptions), \textbf{we therefore claim no
extrapolated exceedance probabilities}. For the same reason the bootstrapped intervals are
themselves not valid confidence intervals (the bootstrap resamples serially dependent
exceedances); Table~\ref{tab:evt} retains the $q_{10^{-9}}$ column only as diagnostic
evidence for why extrapolation is withheld.

What the data does support: the empirical maxima of Table~\ref{tab:tails} with their
cross-session stability (Sec.~\ref{sec:threats}), tails so tight that max/p50 $\le 1.01$ on
every fixture, and, qualitatively, shape estimates that lean negative (bounded-tail
direction) where the fits are most stable (\emph{search-00}, both engines). A naive
block-maxima Gumbel fit on the pooled stream would nevertheless have produced
plausible-looking numbers ($q_{10^{-9}} \approx \SI{\evtGumbelQNine}{ms}$,
$+\evtGumbelExtraPct\%$ over the maximum, insensitive to block size within
\SI{\evtGumbelSpread}{ms}), which is precisely why the diagnostics, not the fit quality,
must decide.
