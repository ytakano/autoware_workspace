\subsection{Search effectiveness}
\label{sec:eval-search}
% Saturation of the analytic bound + 35% kd growth. Table: counters per fixture.
The hill-climb saturates the analytic kernel-evaluation maximum exactly:
$\sumnbr = P \cdot \maxnn \cdot (N_{\mathrm{iter}}{+}1) = 2000 \cdot 64 \cdot 31 =
\num{3968000}$ --- certifying that the hand-built dense-neighbors construction is
kernel-count-optimal --- and then grows the remaining free term \kdnodes{} by a further 35\%
(8.88\,M $\rightarrow$ 12.01\,M, map extent 6 $\rightarrow$ 9 blocks). The frozen union-worst
fixture (\texttt{search\_00}) dominates every measured engine on every percentile.

\subsection{Heap allocations per align}
\label{sec:eval-alloc}
% 11 mallocs/point/pass decomposition + the three layers. Rust proven 0.
The interposer counts decompose as ${\approx}11$ allocations per point per derivative pass on
every fixture (e.g.\ $\num{682248} / (31 \cdot 2000) = 11.0$), locating the source in the
per-point inner loop: a fresh neighbor vector constructed inside the parallel loop body, two
fresh index/distance vectors per radius-search call, and per-query result containers inside
pcl/FLANN (${\approx}8$/point). These ride the allocator's thread-cache fast path --- invisible
on average, but each is a potential lock, page fault, or fragmentation stall inside the
innermost loop. The Rust engine hoists all three layers into a pre-reserved workspace and
measures zero in the same harness, consistent with its allocation-freedom proof.

\subsection{Unit-cost regression}
\label{sec:eval-regression}
% T ~ a*nbr + b*kd + c per engine; R^2 >= 0.997. a: 133 vs 47 ns. Interpretation + caveat on
% the negative intercept (n=6 extrapolation artifact).
Regressing median latency on the (shared) counters yields $R^2 \ge 0.997$ for both engines:
the two-term work model explains the timing. The per-kernel-evaluation cost is
\SI{133}{ns} (C++) vs.\ \SI{47}{ns} (Rust) --- $2.8\times$ --- while C++'s per-kd-node cost is
lower (\SI{26}{ns} vs.\ \SI{38}{ns}); the kd term is second-order on the worst set. (The small
negative Rust intercept is an extrapolation artifact of the $n=6$ fit.)
