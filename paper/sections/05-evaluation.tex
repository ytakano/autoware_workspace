\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}
We use three named measurement configurations. \emph{Isolated} denotes controlled engine
characterization on a pinned core. \emph{Replay} denotes replay under the Linux Completely
Fair Scheduler (CFS), and \emph{Bare-metal} denotes evaluation on the AArch64
\texttt{no\_std} target. The unified serial campaign and the $P$-sweep use Isolated;
the bridge, host-scaling, real-data, and AArch64 subsections report auxiliary,
artifact-pinned experiments. They are not pooled with, or used to adjust, the primary
bounded-build timing results. The host uses \envCpu{} and \envKernel{}. The benchmark core is
isolated with \texttt{isolcpus} and \texttt{nohz\_full}; interrupt-request (IRQ)
affinities are moved, with
three immovable IRQs remaining. Its simultaneous-multithreading (SMT) sibling is offline, and the \envGovernor{}
governor pins its frequency at \SI{3.2}{GHz}. We compile C++ with \envCppCompiler{} using
\texttt{CMAKE\_BUILD\_TYPE=Release} and \envCppReleaseFlags. Rust uses
\envRustCompiler{} with Cargo release defaults \texttt{opt-level=3},
\texttt{lto=false}, and \texttt{codegen-units=16}, plus the workspace override
\texttt{overflow-checks=true}. No target CPU is explicitly selected, and the manifests
record no experiment-specific \texttt{RUSTFLAGS}. Timing binaries use
\texttt{NDT\_BUILD\_TRACED=OFF}; traced analysis binaries are never timed.

The campaign comprises \envSessions{} sessions from distinct boots, with randomized
fixture and engine order. For each fixture and engine, every session records
\envSamplesPerSession{} measured alignments after \envWarmup{} warmups, yielding
\envPooledSamples{} pooled samples per fixture and engine. We pool these samples across
sessions for the warm series; Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-interference} reports matched-size cold
and co-runner (concurrent-interference-workload) series. A \texttt{steady\_clock} measures only the align loop, after the map
and kd-tree have been built once per engine and fixture. A fixed-work calibration guard
rejects a cell whose sustained speed shifts by more than 10\%. Rejected cells are
remeasured, and the pooled data contain accepted cells only. The primary campaign engines
were measured at autoware\_core commit \texttt{\envCommit}. The released data files embed per-run
manifests containing the environment, compiler flags, commits, and fixture and binary hashes.
We use p50 and p99 for the empirical 50th and 99th percentiles, respectively.

\subsection{Stress-witness outcomes}
\label{sec:eval-search}
The domain-informed seed uses the corner-lattice construction represented by
\emph{dense-nbrs}, but raises the source count from 1,500 to the engine limit of 2,000.
It saturates the analytic maximum of the Rust envelope for the common kernel-evaluation
term: $\sumnbr = P \cdot \maxnn \cdot (N_{\mathrm{iter}}{+}1)
= 2000 \cdot 64 \cdot 31 = \runtimeKernelEvalMax{}$. This construction is therefore
kernel-count-optimal under the configured $P_{\max}$, \maxnn, and $I_{\max}$.
The deterministic search then explores the Rust-specific traversal term \kdnodes{};
\emph{search-00} records $\searchZeroKdM\times10^6$ kd-node visits. In the unified
campaign it has the largest C++ observed maximum and p50. Rust p50 is largest on
\emph{pareto-01}, whereas the Rust observed maximum is largest on \emph{pareto-02}.
These orderings apply only to the evaluated fixture set. Table~\ref{tab:counters} lists
the deterministic counters recomputed at the fixed timing commit.

\subsection{Per-input work-trace conformance}
\label{sec:eval-tracecert}
\input{tables/tracecert}

The work-trace conformance check of Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer} was applied to every
frozen fixture and to all \realFrames{} captured real frames. The traced analysis build of
the C++ engine uses an instrumented copy of the engine sources plus a counting FLANN
distance functor; the shipped sources remain byte-identical. It emits a per-pass trace that
the replay compares leg by leg against the Rust engine's mirrored trace. Every quantity is
a deterministic function of the input, so the runs need no controlled timing environment,
and the traced binaries are never used for timing.

Table~\ref{tab:tracecert} shows the fixture leg. The \emph{shape} fields comprise pass
count, per-pass point and neighbor counts, and SHA-256 digests of each point's leaf IDs
after canonical sorting. Shape agrees on all \traceFixtures{} inputs, the line-search loop
is never entered, and the measured C++ pass count equals $N_{\mathrm{iter}}{+}1$ on every
input. This directly establishes equality of the recorded work topology, rather than
inferring it from iterations or output proximity. The independent payload digest, which
also records leaf means and regularized inverse covariances, agrees on
\tracePayloadExactFixtures{} of \traceFixtures{} fixtures. The payload result demonstrates
that shape agreement does not establish equality of all kernel operands.

The C++ neighbor count is the complete result of an uncapped radius search, not a
post-search prefix, whereas the Rust count is the at-most-64 list returned by its capped
search. Shape agreement therefore confirms the bound along the C++ query trajectory.
It does not confirm the counterfactual uncapped count at each Rust query coordinate, which
may differ slightly from its C++ counterpart; that distinction prevents the trace result
from being interpreted as membership in $D$.

The per-pass score agrees to \traceUlpConvMax{} f64 ULP or better on the
well-conditioned fixtures and is bit-identical on \emph{cache-hostile}, but drifts to
$\traceUlpWorstTex$ ULP on the most ill-conditioned geometries. PCL/FLANN returns each
point's neighbors sorted by distance while the port's kd walk returns them in visit order,
so the engines may accumulate the same shape in different orders. This explains why exact
floating-point sums are not required by $E_{\mathrm{shape}}$. SHA-256 makes accidental
digest equality computationally unlikely but does not turn this per-input check into a
formal proof.

The real-data leg applies the same hierarchy to every on-map frame; its exhaustive
partition and timing subsets are reported once in Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-realdata}. Among the
\traceRealWorkGapFrames{} base-only frames, define
$\Delta_{\mathrm{nbr}}=\sumnbr^C-\sumnbr^R$. The median $|\Delta_{\mathrm{nbr}}|$ is
\traceRealWorkGapNbrMed{} and the maximum is \traceRealWorkGapNbrMax, at most
\traceRealWorkGapRelMaxPct\% of a frame's kernel evaluations. Only
\tracePayloadExactOnMap{} on-map frames have an exact payload digest, confirming that
matching shape usually does not imply bit-identical leaf parameters.

\subsection{Heap allocations per align}
\label{sec:eval-alloc}
On fixtures whose radius queries return neighbors, the interposer counts decompose as
${\approx}\allocPerPtPass$ allocations per point per derivative pass. For example,
$\allocWorstPerAlign{} / (31 \cdot 2000) = \allocPerPtPassExact$. This locates the
source in the per-point inner loop: a fresh neighbor
vector constructed inside the parallel loop body, two fresh index/distance vectors per
radius-search call, and approximately eight per-query result containers per point inside PCL/FLANN.
These allocations use the allocator's thread-cache fast path and have low mean cost, but
each is a
potential lock, page fault, or fragmentation stall inside the innermost loop. The Rust
engine hoists all three allocation sites into a pre-reserved workspace and measures zero in
the same harness, consistent with the counting-allocator-verified allocation freedom shown
in Table~\ref{tab:alloc}.

Beyond the hazard reading, these counts provide a separate C++-side consistency check.
The numerator comes from the replay binary containing the unmodified C++ engine via
\texttt{LD\_PRELOAD}, while the denominator
$P \cdot N_{\mathrm{pass}}$ comes from the Rust counters. The ratio is
\allocCertMin--\allocCertMax{} on every fixture whose radius queries return neighbors.
The \allocCertSpreadPct\% spread remains small across geometries whose cost differs by two
orders of magnitude. A single extra C++ derivative pass, which would shift the count by
about 3\% at $N_{\mathrm{pass}} = 31$, would exceed the observed spread. The
one deviation, \emph{subnormal} at \allocCertSparse{}, is itself structural: its
mostly-empty radius searches skip the per-result containers.

\label{sec:eval-regression}
Regression is diagnostic, not unit-cost identification. Across the fixtures and $P$-sweep

\subsection{Scaling with the input size \texorpdfstring{$P$}{P}}
\label{sec:eval-psweep}
We regenerate the Rust-search geometry at six values
$P\in[\psweepPmin{},\psweepPmax{}]$, increasing by a factor of two at each step. The
bounded Rust engine is reconstructed at each point with $P_{\max}=P$; $L_{\max}$ and
$I_{\max}$ remain fixed. Rows above $P=\runtimePMax$ therefore evaluate larger declared
source capacities rather than violating the principal configuration. The counters verify
$N_{\mathrm{iter}}=30$ and $\sumnbr=P\cdot64\cdot31$ at every point, so the common
kernel-evaluation work per source point is invariant. Rust kd traversal per processed point
spans \psweepKdPerPointMin--\psweepKdPerPointMax{} nodes across the six fixtures; it is
measured rather than assumed constant.

Let $T_{\mathrm{obs,max}}(P)$ denote the largest measured alignment time at source-point
count $P$. Linear common-kernel work motivates the descriptive affine model
$T_{\mathrm{obs,max}}(P)=\mathrm{slope}\cdot P+\mathrm{const}$, while traversal,
fixed per-point work, and platform noise remain in the observations. The fit is not a hard
bound.
Table~\ref{tab:psweep} and Fig.~\ref{fig:psweep} show the result: an affine line closely fits
the \emph{max} series, with coefficient of determination $R^2 = \psweepRsqCpp{}$ for C++ and
$\psweepRsqRust{}$ for Rust. The largest absolute residual is
\SI{\psweepResidualCpp}{ms} for C++ at $P=\psweepResidualPCpp{}$ and
\SI{\psweepResidualRust}{ms} for Rust at $P=\psweepResidualPRust{}$; no cache knee is
visible over the tested range. The fitted
observed-maximum slope is
\SI{\psweepSlopeCpp}{\micro\second} for C++ versus
\SI{\psweepSlopeRust}{\micro\second} for Rust
per source point. The slope is a compound empirical cost that includes 64 kernel
evaluations across 31 passes per point, traversal, and fixed per-point work; the experiment
does not identify separate unit costs.

Inverting the fitted observed-maximum line against the 10\,Hz, \SI{100}{ms} budget gives an
\emph{empirical budget crossover} at $P \approx \psweepCrossRust{}$ for Rust and
\psweepCrossCpp{} for C++ under the transferred Rust-search geometry on this host. The Rust
value interpolates within the measured range, whereas the C++ value is a short extrapolation
below its lower endpoint. These point estimates are neither hard admission limits nor WCET
bounds. Table~\ref{tab:psweep-fit} reports the slope and intercept intervals and residuals;
the same protocol is required to obtain a platform-specific crossover on deployment
hardware.

\subsection{Two-tier input-domain analysis}
\label{sec:eval-twotier}
The adversarial fixtures are constructed stress witnesses for the capped-neighbor engine
API; they are not upper bounds on the bounded-neighbor domain $D$ defined in
Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer}. That domain is the
relevant contract for a \texttt{no\_std} kernel deployment in which inputs cross a trust
boundary without production preprocessing validation. When the production
preprocessing contracts of Sec.~\ref{sec:impl-legal} hold, the reachable input space
shrinks. The input geometry of \emph{geom-stress} satisfies those contracts, but its run
uses non-shipped $\epsilon=10^{-10}$ to force 30 iterations. Its observed maximum is
\SI{\legalWorstMaxRust}{ms} for Rust and \SI{\legalWorstMaxCpp}{ms} for C++. These are
measured factors of \legalWorstRatioRust$\times$ and \legalWorstRatioCpp$\times$ below
the transferred Rust-search fixture in Table~\ref{tab:tails}. These are measured
witness-to-witness gaps under stress configurations, not shipped-configuration performance
ratios.

Two caveats scope this factor. It is a \emph{fixture-to-fixture measurement}, not a proven
tightening: \emph{geom-stress} realizes the voxel-center heuristic $K = 8$ per point, while
the production-input domain's analytic ceiling is $K \le 27$ under the assumptions of
Sec.~\ref{sec:implementation}. The contracts alone reduce the parametric neighbor factor
from 64 to 27, a $64/27 \approx 2.4\times$ contraction; a contract-conforming input between
our witness and that ceiling may exist.
The bound is also conditional on the contracts because the engine does not check them.
Table~\ref{tab:preconditions} lists these assumptions.

The mechanism is the $K$ ceiling: disjoint voxel-aligned map tiles cap leaf multiplicity at
1, capping $K$ at 27 instead of 64. This witness realizes
$\bar{K} = \legalWorstKbar$, and the production random-downsample cap fixes
$P \le 1500$. The cost structure also shifts: kernel evaluations drop
$\twoTierKernelDrop\times$, with $\sumnbr$ falling
from $\searchZeroSumNbrM\times10^6$ to $\legalWorstSumNbrK\times10^3$, but time
drops less, because kd-tree traversal, unaffected by the $K$ ceiling, becomes the dominant
term. The counter reduction illustrates the contraction in modeled work, whereas the timing
gap also reflects fixture geometry and configuration and cannot be attributed solely to
input-contract enforcement. Independently, the shipped-configuration search of
Sec.~\ref{sec:impl-legal} reaches the full 30-iteration cap on \emph{shipped-osc}. It costs
\SI{\legalOscMaxRust}{ms} in Rust and \SI{\legalOscMaxCpp}{ms} in C++, with
$\bar{K} = \legalOscKbar$. This establishes iteration-cap reachability under shipped
parameters, not a worst-case timing input. At the same shipped $\epsilon$, the
\emph{geom-stress} input converges before the iteration cap.

\subsection{Real-data replay: cropped-map and degraded-prior stress}
\label{sec:eval-realdata}
To relate the two tiers to real inputs, we used an environment-gated hook at the engine
entry points to capture each NDT input: the map tile, per-align guess, and source cloud. The
capture contains \realFrames{} aligns collected after production preprocessing during a
57-minute urban drive in İstanbul from the Istanbul Open Dataset~\cite{istanbulopendataset},
whose route deliberately traverses LiDAR-hostile structures (a sub-sea tunnel with large
elevation change, long bridges, dense urban canyons). A byte-exact join of every captured cloud against the
recording shows the capture covers the drive's first $\approx$33 minutes, that 5582 clouds
were aligned two or three times by concurrent capture consumers (with different guesses),
and that the capture order is not drive order; per-frame statistics therefore weight some
scan geometries two- or three-fold.
Both engines replayed the resulting buffers offline with
cost counters enabled. The primary replay deliberately discards the recorded
production-filter guesses in favor of a frozen track built from NDT odometry with
constant-velocity extrapolation from a global-navigation-satellite-system (GNSS) seed, so
that the guess stream is independent of the production filter. This track represents
degraded-prior operation, including initialization and filter dropouts; a control replay
with the recorded production guesses appears below.

We treat the experiment as a \emph{cropped-map, degraded-prior stress replay}, useful for
exposing map-boundary numerical branching, stressing initialization and dropout priors,
and identifying port-fidelity failures; it is not a representative nominal-localization
workload over the full recording. The offline benchmark map is cropped, and the
reconstructed trajectory exits it after frame \realOnMapSeqMax{}. Only \realOnMap{}
aligns, distributed across \realSegments{} short
segments in this prefix and covering approximately 2\% of the drive, perform meaningful
matching work. We classify a frame as \emph{on-map} when at least one radius search returns
a neighbor, so $\sumnbr>0$. Every search is empty on the remaining \realOffMap{}
\emph{off-map} frames, making those aligns near-no-ops with a median of
\realOffMapIterMed{} iterations and a C++ p50 of approximately
\SI{\realOffMapCppMed}{ms}.

Table~\ref{tab:realdata} separates these populations. Its first panel reports $P$, maximum
$K$, and the partition by base agreement and trace conformance over all frames. The second reports timing over
all \realOnMap{} on-map frames as a system-level observation and over the
\realShapeOnMap{}-frame $E_{\mathrm{shape}}$ subset as the strict trace-conformant
implementation comparison. The all-frame counts provide capture context, whereas the
near-no-op off-map majority does not characterize localization fidelity.

\input{tables/realdata}

The input counters are consistent with the preprocessing contracts: $P\le\realPMax=1500$
on all \realFrames{} frames. The per-point neighbor count reaches at most
$\realKmaxMax$, which is within the geometric bound of 27 but one above the voxel-center
heuristic of 8. Real centroids can deviate from voxel centers and bring a ninth leaf into
range; 27, rather than 8, is the applicable bound. Under the degraded-prior track, the
iteration cap is reached on \realIterCapPct\% of on-map frames. A control replay of the
same capture with its recorded production extended-Kalman-filter (EKF) guesses --- identical per-frame inputs except
the guess --- separates the prior's contribution: on the \realABCommonN{} frames that are
on-map in both replays the cap share drops from \realABCapDegPct\% to \realABCapProdPct\%,
and over the control replay's own \realProdOnMap{} on-map frames
(\realProdOnMapSegments{} boundary-transit segments spanning the drive's first
\realProdOnMapSpanMin{} minutes) it is \realProdIterCapPct\%. Prior quality therefore
shifts the cap share but does not eliminate it: the cap stays routinely reached with
production-quality priors on this cropped single-tile benchmark map, and the closed-loop
tiled-map replay of the same drive (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-stack}) still reaches it on
\stackRustCapMin--\stackRustCapMax\% (Rust) to \stackCppCapMin--\stackCppCapMax\% (C++)
of aligned frames. Iteration-cap reachability on captured
production-preprocessed geometry thus holds under degraded priors, production priors, and
the closed-loop replay with production-like recovery; the absolute share depends on prior
quality and map configuration.

Under this track, the 10\,Hz deadline is no longer a marginal worst case:
\realOverrunsCpp{} C++ aligns exceed the \SI{100}{ms} budget, whereas Rust has no
overruns. The timing rows report the \emph{Replay} configuration: normal CFS scheduling,
no pinning, one align per frame in production order, and a pinned \SI{3.2}{GHz} reference
clock on all cores. The observed-maximum frame costs \SI{\realCppMax}{ms} in C++ and
\SI{\realRustMax}{ms} in Rust. The overruns represent
\realOverrunsCppOnMapPct\% of the on-map frames, whose C++ median is
\SI{\realCppMedOnMap}{ms}. These Replay observations are not compared numerically with the
primary Isolated maxima because the auxiliary replay uses a separately pinned binary.
The comparison is specific to the recorded reference clock; a production electronic control unit (ECU) may have
different governor and boost behavior. The replay also excludes co-resident Autoware
workloads to isolate align-kernel timing and preserve cross-engine comparability. It
therefore does not establish full-stack deadline compliance. For fixed binaries and inputs, the counter rows do not depend on the timing
configuration.

The conformance levels differ materially. Over all frames,
$|E_{\mathrm{base}}|=\realBaseAll{}$ and
$|E_{\mathrm{shape}}|=\realShapeAll{}$. The exhaustive on-map partition is
\realShapeOnMap{} trace-conformant frames, \realBaseOnly{} base-only frames, and
\realBaseDivergent{} frames outside $E_{\mathrm{base}}$. Thus
\realShapeOnMapPct\% of meaningful matches support the strict work-trace comparison;
\realNotShapeOnMap{} frames, or \realNotShapeOnMapPct\%, do not. The last group contains
\realOutputDivergent{} frames with equal iteration counts but excessive pose or score
differences and \realIterationDivergent{} frames with different iteration counts.
Translation-only synthetic fixtures do not exercise map-tile ordering or rotational
convergence. The evaluated port canonicalizes tile order and preserves the C++ rotation
convention; under that implementation, iteration-count divergence remains on
\realIterationDivergent{} frames, or \realOnMapDivPct\% of on-map frames and
\realDivPctAll\% of all captured frames. Section~\ref{sec:threats} characterizes both
the iteration and equal-iteration output residuals. The traced replay of
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-tracecert} records their per-engine outputs and work shape.
The \realIterationDivergent{} divergences cluster in \realDivSegments{} of
\realSegments{} on-map segments but do not exhibit unusually large work counts:
$\bar{K}$ spans \realDivKbarMin{}--\realDivKbarMax{}, and the iteration count spans
\realDivIterMin--\realDivIterMax{}.
At the maximum-delta frame Rust stops after 17 iterations while C++ reaches 30, amplifying
a small numerical perturbation to \SI{\traceRealDivergentTransDeltaMax}{m}. Such sensitivity
is not language-specific: C++ library, compiler, contraction, or ISA changes can also perturb
accumulation, eigensolution, or convergence. The open-loop replay did not measure that
C++-to-C++ variability.

The pose deltas in Table~\ref{tab:realdata} measure substitutability rather than localization
error because only one engine runs in production and the replay has no ground truth.

\subsection{Closed-loop stack replay with GNSS recovery}
\label{sec:eval-stack}
Stack-level replay extends the comparison to the full NDT--EKF--GNSS loop with
production-like recovery. We replayed the same 57-minute cloud, vehicle-twist (linear and
angular velocity), and GNSS streams twice per engine (C++--Rust--Rust--C++), each run
starting from one shared frozen initial pose on a \SI{\stackMapExtent}{m}-extent map whose
dynamic tiles are loaded within \SI{\stackMapRadius}{m} of the pose estimate, under CFS
with the deployed \stackWorkers-worker configuration (\stackWorkers{} OpenMP threads for
C++, a \stackWorkers-worker Rayon pool for Rust), replaying \stackInputFrames{} clouds at
\SI{10}{Hz}. A watchdog implements the production pose-initializer recovery flow: a GNSS
seed pose, a call to the NDT align service, an EKF reset, and re-enabling of the
localization nodes. It fires after \SI{\stackStarve}{s} without an accepted NDT pose ---
one admitted by the EKF's measurement-acceptance gate --- and we call such a period a
\emph{starvation episode}. Because a recorded drive cannot perform the standstill that
production recovery requires, the watchdog instead dead-reckons the pose returned by the
align service across the multi-second align latency using the live twist stream. As noted
in Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-realdata}, the route traverses LiDAR-hostile structures, so
all rates below are stress-route values.

All \stackReinitTotal{} re-initializations across the four runs succeeded, and no run ever
lost the dynamically loaded map: map tiles remained available on every frame. Starvation
episodes coincide across engines: every run starves at the same \stackSharedEpisodes{}
most difficult route sections (the tunnel approach and one urban section), and
\stackCppOnlyEpisodes{} further sections starve only the C++ runs. Same-engine repeat
bands are narrow and the engine bands do not overlap: \emph{accepted-output coverage} ---
the fraction of input clouds for which the pipeline published an NDT pose that passed the
EKF acceptance gate --- is
\stackCppSurvMin--\stackCppSurvMax\% for C++ versus \stackRustSurvMin--\stackRustSurvMax\%
for Rust, with \stackCppReinitMin--\stackCppReinitMax{} versus
\stackRustReinitMin--\stackRustReinitMax{} re-initializations and iteration-cap shares of

\stackRustExeMedMin--\stackRustExeMedMax{} \si{ms}; the only \SI{100}{ms}-budget
violations are \stackCppOverMin--\stackCppOverMax{} C++ frames per run, all inside

\SI{\stackCppTimeMax}{ms} versus \SI{\stackRustTimeMax}{ms}; no Rust frame exceeds the

the faster engine to tolerate transient match-quality degradations that drive the slower
engine past the starvation threshold. The EKF acceptance-gate decision differs across

\SI{\stackInnovRepeatMin}{m}--\SI{\stackInnovRepeatMax}{m}), consistent with a systematic
engine difference rather than replay noise. With two runs per engine and
feedback-induced trajectory divergence, these remain run-level observations.

embedded evaluation target, we ran the same frozen fixtures through the engine on a

Commit-pinned host expectations, emulator replay, and hardware agree on
\raspiDeviceOk{} of \raspiTotal{} counter tuples. Appendix~\ref{app:crossisa-controls}
describes the controls for fused multiply-add contraction, emulation, and subnormal
arithmetic.

The timing columns of Table~\ref{tab:raspi4} report the \emph{Bare-metal} configuration for
the OS-less target. For each fixture, the series contains
\raspiTimingSamples{} warm and \raspiTimingCold{} cold samples on the warm map and is
bracketed by a fixed-work
calibration spin, the on-target analog of the host campaign's throttle guard and the
relevant hazard over this sustained run. Across approximately 55 minutes, the calibration
drifted at most \raspiCalibDriftPct\%, providing no evidence of clock or thermal throttling.
The worst warm max/p50 difference across all fixtures is

sub-percent spread. These observations support deriving a
platform-specific unit-cost bound, but do not themselves establish one. In
absolute terms the serial configuration is not schedulable at 10\,Hz on this target:
\emph{search-00} costs \SI{\raspiSearchWarmMaxS}{s}, or $\raspiHostFactor\times$ the
x86 host's Isolated maximum. The non-shipped \emph{geom-stress} run costs

separately reports \emph{shipped-osc}. These observations show that the A72 is inadequate
for the measured serial stress runs; they do not define a production-ECU speed requirement
or a shipped-input worst case. On these fixtures, the A72 maximum is
$\raspiHostFactorMin$--$\raspiHostFactorMax\times$ the x86 maximum; the x86 host itself
also misses the \emph{geom-stress} period by $\legalWorstBudgetXRust\times$, as shown in
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-twotier}. The protocol of Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-psweep} can be repeated on
a candidate platform to obtain a platform-specific $P$-sweep. Potential mechanisms for
meeting the deadline include reducing $P$, applying
single-instruction multiple-data (SIMD) vectorization, making
algorithmic changes, and parallelizing the kernel. Section~\ref{sec:eval-parallel} measures

\subsection{Tail diagnostic and rejection of extrapolation}
\label{sec:eval-evt}
For each tail fixture we fitted per-session POT/GPD models to $3{\times}1000$ Isolated
samples and applied the full diagnostic gate. The lag-1--10 autocorrelation function (ACF) reaches
\evtAcfWorst{} rather than the ${\sim}0.03$ expected under independence; consequently
standard independent-sample inference is invalid. In addition,
\evtFitFailures{} of \evtFitChecks{} session-level fits fail the in-sample p99.9 check. These
diagnostics invalidate intervals for $q_{10^{-9}}$, the fitted latency quantile at exceedance
probability $10^{-9}$. We
therefore report no extrapolated exceedance probabilities and retain only the empirical
maxima in Table~\ref{tab:tails}. Table~\ref{tab:evt} in Appendix~\ref{app:evt} gives the
fixture-level diagnostics solely to document this rejection; plausible-looking fits do not
override failed assumptions.
