\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}
All engine-tier timing follows \textbf{Profile B (controlled engine)} of our measurement
policy. Host: \envCpu{}, \envKernel{}; the benchmark core is isolated
(\texttt{isolcpus}/\texttt{nohz\_full}, IRQ affinities moved --- 3 immovable remain), its
SMT sibling offline, and its frequency \emph{pinned} at \SI{3.2}{GHz} under the
\envGovernor{} governor; \envCppCompiler{} (C++, \texttt{-O2}); \envRustCompiler{}
(release). \envSessions{} sessions (\envDate, one across a reboot cycle) with randomized
fixture and engine order; \envIters{} per fixture per engine after \envWarmup{} warmups
(warm series; the cold and co-runner series appear in
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-interference}); \texttt{steady\_clock}, align loop only (map and kd-tree
built once per engine per fixture). A fixed-work calibration guard aborts any series whose
sustained speed shifts by more than 10\% --- it fired twice on transient firmware-level
clamps, which were excluded by re-running the affected series. Engines measured at
autoware\_core commit \texttt{\envCommit}; per-run manifests (environment, compiler flags,
commits, fixture and binary hashes) are embedded in the released data files.

the mean-cost gap on sparse inputs is search-machinery overhead. (No hardware performance
counters are available in the measurement container, so the final split is arithmetic over
microbenchmarks rather than counter-attributed; Sec.~\ref{sec:threats}.)

\subsection{Heap allocations per align}
\label{sec:eval-alloc}
The interposer counts decompose as ${\approx}\allocPerPtPass$ allocations per point per derivative pass on
every fixture (e.g.\ $\allocWorstPerAlign{} / (31 \cdot 2000) = \allocPerPtPassExact$), locating the source in the
per-point inner loop: a fresh neighbor vector constructed inside the parallel loop body, two
fresh index/distance vectors per radius-search call, and per-query result containers inside
pcl/FLANN (${\approx}8$/point). These ride the allocator's thread-cache fast path ---
invisible on average, but each is a potential lock, page fault, or fragmentation stall inside
the innermost loop. The Rust engine hoists all three allocation sites into a pre-reserved
workspace and measures zero in the same harness, consistent with its counting-allocator-verified
allocation freedom (Table~\ref{tab:alloc}).

Beyond the hazard reading, these counts double as an \emph{independent work certificate for
the C++ side} (Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer}): the numerator comes from the untouched
production binary via \texttt{LD\_PRELOAD}, the denominator ($P \cdot N_{\mathrm{pass}}$)
from the Rust counters, and the ratio is \allocCertMin--\allocCertMax{} on every fixture
whose radius queries return neighbors --- a \allocCertSpreadPct\% spread across geometries
whose cost differs by two orders of magnitude, fine enough that a single extra C++ derivative
pass (a ${\sim}3\%$ shift at $N_{\mathrm{pass}} = 31$) could not hide. The one deviation ---
\emph{subnormal} at \allocCertSparse{} --- is itself structural: its mostly-empty radius
searches skip the per-result containers.

\subsection{Cache and interference sensitivity}
\label{sec:eval-interference}
\input{tables/interference}

Two further Profile-B series probe how fragile the warm-series numbers are
(Table~\ref{tab:interference}). The \emph{cold} series (between-sample software cache
eviction) shifts medians by at most \coldWorstPct\% across the whole fixture set --- the
worst-case-shaped inputs are already cache-hostile, so eviction adds little. The
\emph{co-runner} series (one hardware resource stressed at a time from an L3-sharing core)
splits cleanly along the two-tier structure: on \emph{search-00}, whose cost is
kernel-evaluation-bound and cache-resident, no co-runner moves the maximum by more than
${\sim}\coWorstSearch\%$; on \emph{legal-worst}, whose cost is kd-traversal/memory-bound
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-twotier}), the memory-bandwidth co-runner inflates the observed maximum
by up to $+\coWorstLegal\%$ (Rust) while the LLC and FP co-runners remain null. The
deployment tier --- not the engine-tier worst --- is the interference-sensitive one, which
is what its kd-dominated cost structure predicts.

\subsection{Unit-cost regression}
\label{sec:eval-regression}
Regressing median latency on the (shared) counters over the fixtures pooled with the
$P$-sweep points ($n = \regN$ per engine) yields $R^2 \ge \regRsqRust$ for both engines
(Table~\ref{tab:regression}), and the model predicts held-out points with a median
leave-one-out error of \regLooMedCpp\% (C++) / \regLooMedRust\% (Rust). Three diagnostics
scope the fit. First, the regressors are nearly collinear over this design (correlation
\regCorr): costly geometries grow both counters together, so the individual coefficients
share variance and carry the wide bootstrap CIs shown in the table --- the combined
prediction is far better identified than either coefficient alone. Second, the worst
leave-one-out miss is \regLooWorstRust{} (\regLooMaxCpp\%/\regLooMaxRust\%) --- precisely the
neighbor-sparse fixture whose cost the two-term model cannot see because it is dominated by
the per-point constant overhead identified in Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-tails}, corroborating that
analysis. Third, the negative Rust intercept is a fitting artifact whose bootstrap CI covers
zero. What survives these diagnostics: the \emph{model} is predictive, but the individual
coefficients are not statistically resolved on this design --- the point estimates suggest a
per-kernel-evaluation cost of \SI{\regKernelNsCpp}{ns} (C++) vs.\ \SI{\regKernelNsRust}{ns}
(Rust), yet their 95\% CIs overlap, so we treat the coefficient-level gap as indicative
only. The \emph{resolved} engine comparison is the direct per-fixture one
(Table~\ref{tab:tails}: max-latency ratios \tailsRatioMin--\tailsRatioMax, stable to
$\pm\sessRatioSpreadWorst$ across sessions), which needs no decomposition. The kd term is
second-order on the engine-tier worst set, though it dominates the deployment tier
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-twotier}). The apparent per-kernel-evaluation gap is
consistent with the same overhead structure as above --- each C++ evaluation reaches its leaf
through a pointer indirection with the per-query machinery amortized over few neighbors,
where the Rust engine reads a flat leaf array --- though without hardware counters we present
this attribution as arithmetic-plausible rather than counter-proven.

\subsection{Scaling with the input size $P$}
\label{sec:eval-psweep}
In the decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp}, $P$ is the only multiplicative freedom: it scales the
per-iteration sum while leaving $N_{\mathrm{iter}}$, $K$, and $T_{\mathrm{solve}}$ untouched,
predicting an \emph{affine} law $\mathrm{WCET}(P) = \mathrm{slope}\cdot P + \mathrm{const}$.
We validate this with a controlled sweep: the union-worst geometry regenerated at $P \in
[\psweepPmin{}, \psweepPmax{}]$ (six points, $\times 2$ steps), where the counters certify
that per-point work is exactly invariant --- $N_{\mathrm{iter}} = 30$ and $\sumnbr = P \cdot
64 \cdot 31$ at every $P$ --- so time may vary only through $P$ itself.
Table~\ref{tab:psweep} and Fig.~\ref{fig:psweep} show the result: both engines are affine in
$P$ with $R^2 = \psweepRsqCpp{}$ (C++) and $\psweepRsqRust{}$ (Rust) on the \emph{max} series;
no cache knee is visible over the tested range. The fitted worst-case cost is
\SI{\psweepSlopeCpp}{\micro\second} (C++) vs.\ \SI{\psweepSlopeRust}{\micro\second} (Rust)
per source point, consistent with the per-kernel-evaluation costs of
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-regression} (${\approx}64$ kernel evaluations $\times$ 31 passes per
point).

The affine law makes the bound \emph{parametric in the caller's contract}: inverting it
against the \SI{100}{ms} (10\,Hz) budget gives a maximum admissible downsampled-scan size of
$P \approx \psweepPmaxRust{}$ points for the Rust engine (\psweepPmaxCpp{} for C++)
\emph{under the union-worst geometry on this host} --- a number the localization node's
voxel-filter configuration can be audited against directly. (Host-relative; on deployment hardware the same protocol
yields the slope that matters there.)

\subsection{Two-tier bound: engine tier vs.\ deployment tier}
\label{sec:eval-twotier}
The adversarial fixtures bound the engine at its API over the domain $D$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-transfer}) --- the right contract for the
\texttt{no\_std} kernel deployment, where inputs cross a trust boundary unvalidated. When the
production preprocessing contracts hold (Sec.~\ref{sec:impl-legal}), the reachable input
space shrinks, and with it the observed worst case: on \emph{legal-worst}, the hardest
preprocessing-reachable input we constructed, the observed maximum drops to
\SI{\legalWorstMaxRust}{ms} (Rust) / \SI{\legalWorstMaxCpp}{ms} (C++) --- a measured factor
of \legalWorstRatioRust$\times$ / \legalWorstRatioCpp$\times$ below the engine-tier union
worst (Table~\ref{tab:tails}). Two caveats scope this factor. It is a
\emph{fixture-to-fixture measurement}, not a proven tightening: \emph{legal-worst} realizes
the voxel-center heuristic $K = 8$ per point, while the tier's provable ceiling is $K \le 27$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:implementation}) --- the tightening the contracts alone guarantee on the
kernel term is the parametric $64/27 \approx 2.4\times$, and a legal input between our
witness and that ceiling may exist. And it is conditional on the contracts actually holding:
the engine does not check them (Table~\ref{tab:preconditions}). The mechanism is the $K$
ceiling: disjoint voxel-aligned map tiles
cap leaf multiplicity at 1, capping $K$ at 27 --- realized at $\bar{K} = \legalWorstKbar$ by
this witness --- instead of 64, and the production
random-downsample cap fixes $P \le 1500$. Note the shift in the cost structure: kernel
evaluations drop $\twoTierKernelDrop\times$ ($\sumnbr$: $\searchZeroSumNbrM\,\mathrm{M}$
$\rightarrow \legalWorstSumNbrK\,\mathrm{k}$)
but time drops less, because kd-tree traversal --- unaffected by the $K$ ceiling --- becomes
the dominant term of the deployment tier. Read constructively, the pair quantifies what
\emph{enforcing input contracts at the component boundary} buys: validating tile disjointness
and scan-downsample invariants before the align narrows the observed worst case by a measured
$\legalWorstRatioRust\times$ (provably $\ge 2.4\times$ on the kernel term) without touching
the kernel itself. The iteration axis of the
tier is not an artifact of the $\epsilon$ pin: the production-parameterized legal search
(Sec.~\ref{sec:impl-legal}) reaches the full 30-iteration cap on \emph{legal-osc}
(\SI{\legalOscMaxRust}{ms} Rust / \SI{\legalOscMaxCpp}{ms} C++, $\bar{K} = \legalOscKbar$)
--- below \emph{legal-worst}, which therefore remains the tier's hardest measured witness.

\subsection{Real-data replay: contract validation and degraded-prior behavior}
\label{sec:eval-realdata}
To place the tiers against real inputs, we captured every NDT input (map tile, per-align
guess and source cloud) during a replay of a real urban drive (İstanbul; \realFrames{} aligns
over 57 minutes through the production preprocessing) via an environment-gated hook at the
engine's entry points, then replayed the captured frames offline through both engines on
identical buffers with the cost counters enabled. Because the benchmark harness could not
reproduce the production localization filter's prior quality offline, the guess track is a
frozen NDT-odometry reconstruction (constant-velocity extrapolation from a global-navigation-satellite-system (GNSS) seed) ---
\emph{representative of degraded-prior operation} (initialization, filter dropouts), not of
nominal cruising, which the converged node-level baseline shows at 3 iterations per frame.

One consequence of this open-loop track must be stated up front: the offline benchmark map is
a cropped extract, and the reconstructed trajectory exits it after frame \realOnMapSeqMax{} ---
so only \realOnMap{} aligns, a prefix window covering ${\sim}2\%$ of the drive, perform
meaningful matching work. We call a frame \emph{on-map} when at least one of its radius
searches returns a neighbor ($\sumnbr > 0$); on the remaining \realOffMap{} \emph{off-map}
frames every radius search returns empty and the align is a near-no-op (iteration count p50
$= \realOffMapIterMed$, C++ p50 ${\approx}\SI{\realOffMapCppMed}{ms}$). The per-frame
envelope rows of Table~\ref{tab:realdata} therefore use the on-map subset, while the $P$ and
max-$K$ contract checks --- which do not depend on map coverage --- use all frames.

Table~\ref{tab:realdata} shows the distributions; four observations. First, the preprocessing
contracts hold empirically: $P \le \realPMax = 1500$ on all \realFrames{} frames. Second, the
per-point neighbor count reaches at most $\realKmaxMax$: within the per-tile geometric bound
of 27, but \emph{one above} the voxel-center heuristic of 8 --- real centroids deviate from
voxel centers, pulling a ninth leaf into range; the load-bearing bound is the 27, not the 8.
Third, under degraded priors the iteration cap is \emph{routinely} reached on real geometry
(\realIterCapPct\% of on-map frames),
corroborating the deployment tier's iteration axis with real data. The timing rows are
\textbf{Profile-A} results under the measurement policy (normal CFS scheduling, no pinning,
one align per frame in production order, all cores at the pinned \SI{3.2}{GHz} reference
clock): the real worst frame costs \SI{\realCppMax}{ms} (C++) vs.\ \SI{\realRustMax}{ms}
(Rust). At the 10\,Hz deadline this is no longer a grazing worst case:
\realOverrunsCpp{} C++ aligns --- \realOverrunsCppOnMapPct\% of the on-map frames, with an
on-map C++ median of \SI{\realCppMedOnMap}{ms} --- exceed the \SI{100}{ms} budget under this
degraded-prior track, while Rust never does; the Rust maximum sits
$\realMarginDeploy\times$ below the deployment-tier witness and
${\sim}\realMarginEngine\times$ below the engine tier (tier maxima are Profile-B values;
the comparison is justified for Rust by the bridge experiment's
$\bridgeRustInflMin$--$\bridgeRustInflMax\times$ A/B ratios,
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-bridge}). Two scope notes: a production ECU's governor and boost
behavior may differ from the pinned reference clock (recorded, not assumed), and no
co-resident Autoware workload ran during the replay --- a full-stack co-residency run
remains future work. The counter rows are environment-independent. Fourth, equal work holds on
\realMatch{} of \realFrames{} frames overall (\realMatchPct\%) --- but the honest denominator
is the on-map subset: all \realDivergences{} divergences fall on on-map frames, so the
equal-work rate over the frames doing meaningful matching work is \realOnMapMatchPct\%
(\realOnMapMatch{} of \realOnMap).

\subsection{Bridge experiment: controlled vs.\ production profiles}
\label{sec:eval-bridge}
The two profiles are linked by a mandatory bridge experiment (our measurement policy): the
same five frozen inputs --- the union-worst, both deployment-legal witnesses, and the
slowest and median-cost real frames (frozen from the drive capture) --- replayed under both
profiles at the same \SI{3.2}{GHz} reference clock, same harness, differing only in the
environment. Table~\ref{tab:bridge} reports medians, maxima, and the descriptive
interference-inflation ratio (production over controlled).

\input{tables/bridge}

Two findings. For the \textbf{Rust} engine the ratio is
$\bridgeRustInflMin$--$\bridgeRustInflMax\times$ on every input: the controlled maxima
transfer to the production CFS configuration essentially unchanged (on an otherwise-idle
host --- co-resident load is a separate axis, probed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-interference}). For the \textbf{C++} engine the ratio is
\emph{uniformly below one} ($\bridgeCppInflMin$--$\bridgeCppInflMax\times$): the controlled
environment is not engine-neutral. A same-boot control isolates the cause: pinned to the
isolated core the C++ align costs \SI{194.9}{ms} (\emph{legal-osc}) against \SI{165.8}{ms}
on a non-isolated core of the same boot, same pin --- while Rust, in the same binary, is
unchanged --- so the per-core isolation flags themselves
(\texttt{isolcpus}/\texttt{nohz\_full}/\texttt{rcu\_nocbs}) tax the C++ engine's per-align
constant by \SI{\bridgeCppTaxMinMs}{}--\SI{\bridgeCppTaxMaxMs}{ms} on the measured inputs
(sub-flag attribution not pursued; the zero-allocation engine's immunity points at the
allocator/per-query machinery). Frequency, core choice, SMT-sibling state, transparent
hugepages, and fault counts were ruled out by direct A/B tests; a fixed-work calibration
spin runs marginally \emph{faster} on the isolated core.
Consequently the Profile-B C++ maxima are \emph{conservative} for this production
configuration, every cross-engine conclusion of Table~\ref{tab:tails} survives under
Profile A with wider margins, and the policy's caution that scheduler and interference
costs must not be assumed additive or constant is borne out in the strongest possible way:
for one engine the ``interference inflation'' is not even ${\ge}1$.

\subsection{On-target counter transfer (AArch64)}
\label{sec:eval-raspi4}
\input{tables/raspi4}

To validate the cross-ISA leg of the methodology (Sec.~\ref{sec:meth-crossisa}) on the
actual deployment class of hardware, we ran the same frozen fixtures through the engine on a
Raspberry Pi~4 (Cortex-A72) under the bare-metal \texttt{no\_std} kernel: an async task
parses the embedded fixture bytes, performs one counted align each, and asserts the counters
against the host values (Table~\ref{tab:raspi4}). The result: every counter of every fixture
transfers \emph{exactly} (\raspiExact{} of \raspiTotal) --- reached via a detour that
stress-tested the certificate. The \emph{first} hardware run reported 7 of 8 against the
expectation table then embedded in the kernel app (that log is preserved in the repository
history): on \emph{search-01} the measured work differed by
$\sumnbr\,{+}\raspiNbrDelta$ / $\kdnodes\,{-}\raspiKdDelta$
--- deterministically, and bit-identically under QEMU-TCG emulation and on the Cortex-A72
silicon. After the ISA-level mechanisms were excluded (no fused-multiply-add instructions in
the kernel image; the FPU at IEEE defaults, with the \emph{subnormal} fixture transferring
exactly), commit bisection located the true cause \emph{off-target}: the embedded
expectations had been transcribed from a host snapshot that predated one of the two
port-fidelity fixes of Sec.~\ref{sec:threats} (the pcl f32 transform-association mirror),
which shifts this knife-edge fixture's work by \raspiNbrDelta{} evaluations in
\raspiMismatchNbrM\,M. Re-baselining against the fixed engine reconciles host, emulator, and
hardware exactly: the re-captured hardware run (Table~\ref{tab:raspi4}) reports
\raspiDeviceOk{} of \raspiTotal{} on-device, with counters bit-identical between the two
hardware runs. No measurable ISA dependence remains, and the flag was the certificate
doing its job at parts-per-million resolution --- the counters double as a behavioral
fingerprint of the engine \emph{version}, which is why baselines must be pinned to engine
commits (the artifact-manifest discipline of Sec.~\ref{sec:threats}).

The timing columns of Table~\ref{tab:raspi4} are a \textbf{Profile-C} series (an amendment
to the measurement policy for OS-less targets): per fixture, \raspiTimingSamples{} warm and
\raspiTimingCold{} cold samples on the warm map, the series bracketed by a fixed-work
calibration spin --- the on-target analog of the host campaign's throttle guard, and the
relevant hazard here, since the Pi~4 throttles under sustained load. Over the
${\sim}55$-minute run the calibration drifted at most \raspiCalibDriftPct\%: no thermal
throttling. The distributions are strikingly tight --- worst warm max/p50 across all
fixtures is ${+}\raspiSpreadWorstPct\%$, and the cold series (an 8\,MiB eviction sweep
before every sample) sits at most ${+}\raspiColdDeltaWorstPct\%$ above the warm maximum ---
tighter than the isolated-host Profile-B series, as expected with no OS, one active core,
and a firmware-fixed clock. This is precisely the platform class where the factorization of
Sec.~\ref{sec:threats} pays off: the work bound transfers exactly (the counters), the
per-unit cost is nearly deterministic (sub-percent spread), so
$\text{work bound} \times \text{unit-cost bound}$ approaches an analytical WCET. In
absolute terms the engine-tier worst costs \SI{\raspiSearchWarmMaxS}{s} on the Cortex-A72
($\raspiHostFactor\times$ the x86 host's Profile-B maximum) --- far past any 10\,Hz budget,
quantifying how much headroom the deployment tier's input contracts must buy on
embedded-class silicon (per-fixture values in Table~\ref{tab:raspi4}).

\subsection{Extreme-value analysis: what the diagnostics permit}
\label{sec:eval-evt}
\input{tables/evt}

With $3{\times}1000$ Profile-B samples per tail-claim fixture, we ran the
measurement-based-\pwcet{} analysis in full: peaks-over-threshold fits of a generalized
Pareto distribution by maximum likelihood, per session (pooling would mix the documented
cross-session level shifts), with a threshold sweep, \evtBoot{} bootstrap draws for CIs,
and the policy's diagnostic checklist. \emph{The diagnostics gate the conclusion}
(Table~\ref{tab:evt}): the sample series carry strong serial dependence --- lag-1..10
autocorrelation magnitudes up to \evtAcfWorst{} where an i.i.d.\ series of this length
would show ${\sim}0.03$ --- because on an isolated, frequency-pinned core the residual
variability is a slowly wandering band of ${\sim}0.1$--$0.5\%$, not independent noise. The
