\section{Problem: WCET of an Iterative Scan Matcher}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{The NDT align kernel}
\ndt{} registration~\cite{biber2003ndt,magnusson2009thesis} represents map voxels by
regularized Gaussian means and covariances. Each Newton step transforms all $P$ scan points,
radius-searches a kd-tree of nearby voxel centroids, accumulates score, gradient, and a
$6{\times}6$ Hessian over point--leaf pairs, solves the Newton system, and updates the
six-degree-of-freedom pose. Execution stops at update threshold $\epsilon$ or the
30-iteration cap.

We study the serial Autoware multigrid engine, based on the Point Cloud Library (PCL) and derived from
\texttt{ndt\_omp}~\cite{autoware,ndtomp}. Dynamic map tiles retain separate voxel grids,
while one kd-tree spans all leaf centroids; overlapping tiles can therefore multiply
neighbors. The \emph{engine} is the align kernel and map structures. The ROS \emph{node}
provides scans, initial guesses, and tiles and consumes poses; it is outside the timing
kernel.

\subsection{Execution-time decomposition and notation}
\label{sec:decomposition}
For one align invocation, let $T_{\mathrm{model}}$ denote the modeled kernel time, $P$ the
number of source points, and $p\in\{1,\ldots,P\}$ a source-point index. Let
$N_{\mathrm{iter}}$ be the number of completed Newton updates and $N_{\mathrm{pass}}$ the
number of derivative-evaluation passes. For point $p$, $T_{\mathrm{search}}(p)$ bounds one
radius search and $K(p)$ bounds the number of returned leaves. Finally,
$T_{\mathrm{kernel}}$ bounds one point--leaf score, gradient, and Hessian evaluation, and
$T_{\mathrm{solve}}$ bounds one Newton solve and pose update. We use the following
parametric decomposition:
\begin{equation}
 T_{\mathrm{model}}\le N_{\mathrm{pass}}\sum_{p=1}^{P}
 \bigl(T_{\mathrm{search}}(p)+K(p)T_{\mathrm{kernel}}\bigr)
 +N_{\mathrm{iter}}T_{\mathrm{solve}}.
 \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
The pass index is suppressed by taking $T_{\mathrm{search}}(p)$ and $K(p)$ as
maxima over the passes of the invocation. These $T_*$ terms are symbolic unit-cost bounds,
not coefficients inferred from the timing measurements. We write
$K=\max_{1\le p\le P}K(p)$ for the maximum neighbor multiplicity over the complete
invocation.

The shipped configuration disables line-search derivative refinement, so one initial pass
plus one pass per Newton step gives $N_{\mathrm{pass}}=N_{\mathrm{iter}}+1\le31$.
The bounded Rust engine enforces $P\le P_{\max}$,
$N_{\mathrm{leaves}}\le L_{\max}$, and $K(p)\le\maxnn=64$. Here, $P_{\max}$ is
the configured source-point limit, $N_{\mathrm{leaves}}$ is the number of active map
leaves indexed by the kd-tree, $L_{\max}$ is its configured leaf limit, and \maxnn{} is
the per-query neighbor cap. A kd traversal visits at most $N_{\mathrm{leaves}}$ nodes per
query. The principal evaluation configuration uses $P_{\max}=\runtimePMax$ and
$L_{\max}=\runtimeLeavesMax$: production replay observes at most 1500 scan points, while
the engine stress fixtures use 2000. Section~\ref{sec:eval-psweep} separately varies the
configured $P_{\max}$. Table~\ref{tab:preconditions} records the principal limits and
violation behavior.

We count total point--leaf evaluations as \sumnbr, total Rust kd nodes as \kdnodes, and
$\bar K=\sumnbr/(P N_{\mathrm{pass}})$. For C++, we aggregate the Fast Library for Approximate Nearest Neighbors (FLANN)
traversal events as
$\flannevals=\Sigma_{\mathrm{dist}}^{C}+\Sigma_{\mathrm{plane}}^{C}$, the sum of
distance and split-plane evaluations. Thus \sumnbr{} is included in the work-trace
conformance check, whereas \kdnodes{} and \flannevals{} count different events and their
fitted costs are engine-specific. A diagnostic timing model adds fixed per-point and
$6{\times}6$ solve terms. Equation~\eqref{eq:decomp} is therefore a parametric envelope for
the modeled kernel work, not a bound on total elapsed time: fixed setup, point
transformation, memory-system interference, and operating-system delay require separate
platform-specific bounds.

\subsection{Why work bounds need timing evidence}
Individual maxima need not be jointly reachable, so constructed inputs are witnesses, not
upper bounds. Cache, allocator, and floating-point costs are absent from the work model.
In particular, C++ performs about 11 heap allocations per point per derivative pass on
neighbor-returning fixtures. This is a known input-proportional latency path whose cost is
not bounded by the work model;
removing it eliminates one such path but does not by itself establish a finite latency bound
for the kernel or the surrounding Linux system.
