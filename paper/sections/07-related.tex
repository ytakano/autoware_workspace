\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Static WCET analysis}
Static analyzers in the aiT tradition compute certified bounds from abstract cache and pipeline
models~\cite{ferdinand2001ait}; Wilhelm et al.~\cite{wilhelm2008wcet} survey the field. These
methods excel on control-dominated code whose loop bounds are program constants or annotatable
invariants. In our kernel, the Newton iteration count, per-point neighbor multiplicity, and
kd-tree traversal length are properties of the \emph{input geometry}. Static analysis alone
can only return the product of the individual maxima in Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp}, with no
evidence that any input realizes it. Our Layer~1 keeps the
spirit of the static bound but enforces each term by construction and discharges it by
property-based testing, while Layers~2--3 supply witness inputs and platform timing.

\paragraph{Measurement-based and probabilistic timing analysis}
MBPTA applies extreme value theory to measured execution times to extrapolate
\pwcet{}~\cite{edgar2001gumbel,cucu2012mbpta}; Cazorla et al.~\cite{cazorla2019mbpta} give a
survey. A central limitation is \emph{representativity}: EVT bounds only the
behaviors exercised by the measurement protocol, which has driven work on randomized platforms and
measurement protocols~\cite{lima2017evt}, statistical estimation and validation
procedures~\cite{hansen2009statistical}, and reusable tooling~\cite{reghenzani2018chronovise}.
We attack representativity from the input side: instead of sampling ``representative'' inputs,
we \emph{search} for adversarial ones with a deterministic, platform-independent fitness, and
certify for the dominant term that the search reached the analytic maximum. On the frozen
extreme inputs we then run the POT/MLE machinery with its diagnostic checklist, and we report
that diagnostics determine whether extrapolation is reported, as detailed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-evt}.

\paragraph{Search-based worst-case input generation}
Evolutionary testing of timing constraints dates to Wegener et
al.~\cite{wegener1997testing,wegener2001comparison}, who evolve test data with \emph{measured
execution time} as fitness and note the complementarity with static analysis. Wall-clock
fitness is noisy, machine-specific, and unreproducible across platforms. Our fitness is a
vector of deterministic algorithmic cost counters: the search result is a property of the
algorithm rather than of the measurement machine, runs are exactly reproducible from a seed,
and the outcome can be checked against the analytic bound. The saturation certificate of
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-search} has no analogue under time-based fitness. The counters also
enable cross-language transfer: a counter-extremal input found on the instrumented port is
replayed on the production engine, with common semantic work trace-certified per input.
Engine-specific traversal and timing remain separate, so the shared witness need not
stress both implementations equally.

\paragraph{Timing analysis of ROS-based systems}
Casini et al.~\cite{casini2019ros2} bound end-to-end response times of ROS~2 processing chains
under reservation-based scheduling, treating per-callback WCETs as given inputs. Such analyses
are complementary to ours: we bound the dominant callback kernel, the \ndt{} align, that
those chain analyses consume. We also remove per-point heap allocation and other structural
hazards that would otherwise make the per-callback WCET assumption fragile.

\paragraph{Rust in real-time and safety-critical systems}
Rust's ownership model underpins systems such as the Tock embedded
OS~\cite{levy2017tock}, and RustBelt~\cite{jung2018rustbelt} gives machine-checked foundations
for its safety claims. In safe Rust, ownership, lifetime, and aliasing rules discharge
memory-safety and data-race-freedom obligations that an equivalent C++ implementation would
need to establish through its own design and verification evidence. This advantage does not
establish WCET: panic-freedom, allocation-freedom, recursion-freedom, and bounded loops on
the hot path remain separate requirements. We show these can be made lint- and test-enforced
properties of a production kernel. In this case study, a high-fidelity,
per-input-certified port is not just a migration artifact but an analysis instrument: its
safe core narrows the verification surface while carrying the instrumentation burden for
both languages.

\paragraph{NDT scan matching}
\ndt{} registration originates with Biber and Stra{\ss}er~\cite{biber2003ndt} and was extended
to 3D by Magnusson~\cite{magnusson2009thesis}. The engine studied here is Autoware's
production localization matcher~\cite{autoware}, a multigrid variant of the multi-threaded
\texttt{ndt\_omp} implementation~\cite{ndtomp}. Prior work on these implementations reports
average-case throughput. We are not aware of a published WCET-oriented analysis of an \ndt{} engine.
