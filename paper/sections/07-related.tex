\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Static WCET analyzers derive certified time bounds from program-flow and hardware
models~\cite{ferdinand2001ait,wilhelm2008wcet}. Parametric WCET analysis retains
input-dependent quantities in symbolic formulas~\cite{bygde2008parametric,huber2012parametric};
static analysis is therefore not limited to multiplying independent maxima. In this NDT
kernel, however, Newton iterations, neighbor multiplicity, and kd-tree traversal are jointly
induced by floating-point geometry, while a useful bound must be tied to a realizable input.
Our parameterized work envelope is not a processor-time analysis: it enforces and
property-tests structural limits, constructs witnesses for selected terms, and measures them.
It complements rather than replaces certified static hardware analysis.

Measurement-based probabilistic timing analysis (MBPTA) applies extreme value theory (EVT)
to execution-time samples to infer probabilistic worst-case execution time
(\pwcet{})~\cite{edgar2001gumbel,cucu2012mbpta,cazorla2019mbpta}. Its validity depends on both
test coverage~\cite{law2016coverage} and the statistical structure of the measurements.
Lima et al.~\cite{lima2016careful} discuss failure modes in applying EVT to execution times;
Guet et al.~\cite{guet2017representativity} analyze serial dependence and multi-mode tasks,
while Bozhko et al.~\cite{bozhko2023pwcet} formalize the guarantees ascribed to \pwcet{}.
Classical dependent-EVT work formalizes local dependence and the extremal
index~\cite{leadbetter1983extremes}; threshold-exceedance and declustering methods address
serial dependence and clustered extremes~\cite{davison1990threshold,ferro2003clusters}.
These concerns apply directly here because geometry induces distinct modes and the frozen
series exhibit substantial autocorrelation. We address input coverage by constructing stress
fixtures and ranking them with deterministic counters, but the generator still covers only a
parameterized family. The POT/GPD fits fail the predeclared dependence or adequacy gates in
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-evt}; consequently, we report observed maxima rather than an extrapolated
\pwcet{} estimate.

Search-based timing-stress generation dates to Wegener et
al.~\cite{wegener1997testing,wegener2001comparison}, who use measured execution time and
describe evolutionary testing as complementary to static analysis. Because wall-clock
fitness is noisy and platform-specific, we rank fixtures with a language-common kernel-work
counter and a Rust-specific traversal counter. For a fixed Rust implementation, this ranking
is timing-independent. Only the common counter is shown to reach its structural ceiling.
Replaying Rust-search fixtures on C++ tests per-input work-trace conformance, but establishes
neither a C++ traversal extremum nor a time bound.

ROS~2 chain analyses use callback WCETs to bound end-to-end response
times~\cite{casini2019ros2}. Multi-executor analysis covers reaction time and data age in an
autonomous-driving case study~\cite{teper2024ros2}, while a recent survey spans executor,
communication, tracing, and accelerator timing~\cite{casini2026ros2survey}. ROS~2
autonomous-driving architectures and ROS~2--Adaptive AUTOSAR integration add vehicle-level
deployment context~\cite{bonci2026ros2av,hong2024autosar}. We work below that level, excluding
map loading, scheduling, communication, and downstream callbacks. Our callback evidence is
conditional and platform-specific, not a certified callback time or 10-Hz end-to-end
guarantee.

Rust's ownership model supports resource-constrained systems such as Tock
OS~\cite{levy2017tock}, while RustBelt~\cite{jung2018rustbelt} gives machine-checked
foundations for core language safety claims. Safe Rust narrows memory-safety and data-race
obligations in our safe core; it does not establish WCET. Panic-, allocation-, recursion-,
and loop-boundedness remain separate, and unsafe code, the C ABI, and dependencies remain
trusted. Here the port serves both as an engine and as a controlled instrumentation surface.

NDT registration originates with Biber and Stra{\ss}er~\cite{biber2003ndt} and was extended
to 3D by Magnusson~\cite{magnusson2009thesis}. Autoware's production matcher~\cite{autoware}
is a multigrid derivative of the parallel \texttt{ndt\_omp} implementation~\cite{ndtomp}.
Prior work demonstrates real-time NDT pose correction and reports timing across map
resolutions~\cite{lim2020realtimendt}; it primarily evaluates throughput, accuracy, and
robustness. LIO-SAM and FAST-LIO2 likewise emphasize accuracy, rate, and computational
efficiency in LiDAR--inertial localization~\cite{shan2020liosam,xu2022fastlio2}. We instead
study conditional structural limits, stress witnesses, and cross-language work traces for one industrial align kernel, without proposing a localization
method or a hard deadline guarantee. We are not aware of a published WCET-oriented NDT
analysis with this combination.
