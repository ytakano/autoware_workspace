\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The case study separates analysis of a data-dependent industrial kernel into three
obligations: enforce and test a work envelope, generate stress witnesses with deterministic
counters, and measure frozen inputs. A domain-informed construction attains the common
kernel-count ceiling, while two archived candidates exercise high Rust traversal counts at
lower common-kernel work. The port removes the per-point allocation hazard within
reserved capacities. Rust has the
smaller observed maximum on every warm-series fixture and in
\coRustLowerCells{} of \coPairedCells{} equal-sample comparisons across the warm, cold,
and co-runner series. These timing results remain empirical.

Per-input traces compare a precisely defined work shape without equating leaf payloads,
implementation-specific traversal, or latency. All frozen synthetic fixtures satisfy the
work-trace criterion, although their payload digests differ. The auxiliary cropped-map,
degraded-prior replay provides a more restrictive result: only \realOnMap{} frames perform
meaningful matching, \realShapeOnMap{} satisfy both the base and work-trace criteria, and
\realBaseDivergent{} fall outside the base criterion. Among the latter,
\realIterationDivergent{} take different iteration paths. The replay therefore exposes
port-fidelity and map-boundary sensitivity rather than nominal operation.

Rust is not necessary for these bounds. Its safe core narrows memory-safety and race
obligations so validation can focus on equivalence, numerics, work, and timing; FFI, unsafe
code, and dependencies remain trusted.

The auxiliary NDT--EKF--GNSS replay extends the evidence to feedback and recovery on the
stress route: both engines starve at the same most difficult sections and
every GNSS re-initialization succeeds, while the engine bands stay disjoint
(accepted-output coverage \stackCppSurvMin--\stackCppSurvMax\% for C++ versus
\stackRustSurvMin--\stackRustSurvMax\% for Rust) and the only \SI{100}{ms} overruns are
C++ frames inside recovery windows. It does not establish production readiness.
Operational evaluation requires the production ECU, ground truth, co-resident loads, and
end-to-end \SI{10}{Hz} acceptance-gate and innovation measurements. Host parallelism places the
non-shipped \emph{geom-stress} run below the period with two cores; it establishes neither
shipped deadline compliance nor multi-core WCET. Stronger timing claims require either a
deployment cost bound or an independence-preserving measurement protocol.
