\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The case study separates analysis of a data-dependent industrial kernel into three
obligations: enforce and test a work envelope, search deterministic counters for stress
witnesses, and measure frozen inputs. The search saturates the Rust envelope's maximum for
the common kernel-evaluation term and separately stresses Rust kd-tree traversal. The port
removes the per-point allocation hazard within reserved capacities. Across the union of the
primary and counter-Pareto frontier campaigns, the engine-specific observed maxima are
\SI{\combinedFixtureMaxCpp}{ms} for C++ and \SI{\combinedFixtureMaxRust}{ms} for Rust;
these timing results remain empirical.

Per-input traces compare a precisely defined work shape without equating leaf payloads,
implementation-specific traversal, or latency. All frozen synthetic fixtures satisfy the
work-trace criterion, although their payload digests differ. The cropped-map,
degraded-prior replay provides a more restrictive result: only \realOnMap{} frames perform
meaningful matching, \realShapeOnMap{} satisfy both the base and work-trace criteria, and
\realBaseDivergent{} fall outside the base criterion. Among the latter,
\realIterationDivergent{} take different iteration paths. The replay therefore exposes
port-fidelity and map-boundary sensitivity but does not characterize representative nominal
localization over the complete recording.

Rust is not necessary for these bounds. Its safe core narrows memory-safety and race
obligations so validation can focus on equivalence, numerics, work, and timing; FFI, unsafe
code, and dependencies remain trusted.

The NDT--EKF replay extends the evidence to rotation, quality gates, innovations, and
feedback but does not establish production readiness: C++ stayed below \SI{46.8}{ms}, yet
same-engine runs diverged, left the available map, and did not recover after their maxima.
Operational evaluation requires the production ECU, ground truth, co-resident loads, and
end-to-end \SI{10}{Hz} gate and innovation measurements. Host parallelism establishes
feasibility rather than multi-core WCET; stronger timing claims require either a deployment
cost bound or an independence-preserving measurement protocol.
