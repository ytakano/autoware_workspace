\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This case study shows how a high-fidelity, per-input-certified port can turn WCET analysis of a
data-dependent industrial kernel into
a tractable pipeline: a structural work bound enforced and
property-tested in the port, counter-extremal inputs found by a deterministic
counter-guided search with an exact saturation certificate for the dominant term, and
cross-language equal-work measurement
requiring no instrumentation of the production C++ sources. The resulting evidence is
\emph{layered}: enforced by construction and property-tested on the work axis, parametric in
the caller's contract through a $P$-affine, two-tier model, and empirical only in the
time-per-operation factor. On every adversarial fixture
the Rust port meets or beats the C++ engine's observed maximum while eliminating its per-point
heap-allocation hazard, and the per-input equal-work certification exposed port-fidelity gaps
that conventional differential testing had missed.

Rust is not a prerequisite for the work bounds or real-time hardening reported here; an
equivalent C++ redesign is possible. Its role in this case study is to discharge the safe
core's memory-safety and data-race-freedom obligations through the language, so that
project-specific validation can concentrate on equivalence, numerical behavior, bounded
work, and timing. That reduction stops at the FFI, unsafe code, and trusted-dependency
boundaries identified in Sec.~\ref{sec:threats}.

\paragraph{Future work}
On the bare-metal AArch64 evaluation target, both legs are now exercised. The counters
transfer exactly, and Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-raspi4} reports sub-percent spread in the
Profile-C timing series. What remains there is the multi-core interference analog, with
kernel tasks as co-runners, and a certified per-operation cost bound that would promote
$\text{work} \times \text{unit cost}$ to the analytical WCET discussed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:threats}. The serial engine is not schedulable at 10\,Hz on that target, so
the production ECU should be audited with the same on-target $P$-sweep protocol. On the
host, the port's order-preserving, numerically identical rayon backend parallelizes the
derivative passes. Section~\ref{sec:eval-parallel} reports approximately
\parRustSpeedupFour$\times$ at four cores and shows that two cores bring the
deployment-tier witness within the \SI{100}{ms} budget. The full POT/MLE EVT machinery was
run and rejected by its own diagnostics in Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-evt}; reviving extrapolation needs an independence-preserving
measurement protocol, not a better fit. Finally, the counter-guided transfer methodology is a candidate for other data-dependent
Autoware kernels with comparable structural counters.
