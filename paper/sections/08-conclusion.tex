\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Within the evaluated scope, the frozen synthetic fixtures establish work-shape conformance
but not payload equality. In the cropped-map, degraded-prior stress replay, only
\realOnMap{} frames perform meaningful matching; \realShapeOnMap{} satisfy both the base
and work-shape criteria, while \realBaseDivergent{} fall outside the base criterion.
Among the latter, \realIterationDivergent{} take different iteration paths. These results
expose port-fidelity and map-boundary sensitivity but do not characterize representative
nominal-localization behavior over the full 57-minute recording.

The case study turns analysis of a data-dependent industrial kernel into three separable
obligations: enforce and test a work envelope, search deterministic counters for stress
witnesses, and measure frozen inputs. Per-input traces compare a precisely defined work
shape without equating leaf payloads, implementation-specific traversal, or latency. The
search saturates the shared kernel term and separately stresses Rust kd traversal. C++
serves as the port-conformance and timing baseline on transferred fixtures; it is not the
target of the traversal search.

The intended result is a real-time-oriented Rust NDT engine with explicit work limits and
measured timing evidence for its critical path. The port removes the per-point allocation
hazard within reserved capacities and has no larger observed maximum on the shared fixtures.
Timing remains empirical.

Rust is not necessary for these bounds. Its safe core narrows memory-safety and race
obligations so validation can focus on equivalence, numerics, work, and timing; FFI, unsafe
code, and dependencies remain trusted.

The NDT--EKF replay extends the evidence to rotation, quality gates, innovations, and
feedback but does not establish production readiness: C++ stayed below 46.8 ms, yet
same-engine runs diverged, left the available map, and did not recover after their maxima.
Operational evaluation
requires the production ECU, ground truth, co-resident loads, and end-to-end 10 Hz gate and
innovation measurements. Host parallelism is feasibility, not multi-core WCET; stronger
claims require a deployment cost bound or an independence-preserving protocol.
