\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Within the evaluated scope, port fidelity is supported by the frozen synthetic fixtures and
by the \realTraceOnMap{} trace-certified on-map frames, not by the full 57-minute recording.
The open-loop real-data experiment is a cropped-map, degraded-prior stress replay: only
\realOnMap{} frames perform meaningful matching, and \realIterationDivergent{} of them take
different iteration paths. It exposes port-fidelity failures and map-boundary sensitivity
but does not characterize representative nominal-localization behavior.

The case study turns analysis of a data-dependent industrial kernel into three separable
obligations: enforce and test a work envelope, search deterministic counters for stress
witnesses, and measure frozen inputs. Per-input traces transfer common semantic work to
unmodified C++ without equating implementation-specific traversal or latency. The search
saturates the dominant kernel term; the Rust port removes the per-point allocation hazard
within reserved capacities and has no larger observed maximum on shared fixtures. Timing
remains empirical.

Rust is not necessary for these bounds. Its safe core narrows memory-safety and race
obligations so validation can focus on equivalence, numerics, work, and timing; FFI, unsafe
code, and dependencies remain trusted.

The NDT--EKF replay extends the evidence to rotation, quality gates, innovations, and
feedback but does not establish production readiness: C++ stayed below 46.8 ms, yet
same-engine runs diverged, left the available map, and did not recover after their maxima.
Operational evaluation
requires the production ECU, ground truth, co-resident loads, and end-to-end 10 Hz gate and
innovation measurements. Host parallelism is feasibility, not multi-core WCET; stronger
claims require a deployment cost bound or an independence-preserving protocol.
