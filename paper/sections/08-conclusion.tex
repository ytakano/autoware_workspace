\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We showed that a high-fidelity, per-input-certified port turns WCET analysis of a
data-dependent industrial kernel into
a tractable, largely automated pipeline: a structural work bound enforced and
property-tested in the port, counter-extremal inputs found by a deterministic
counter-guided search (with an exact saturation certificate for the dominant term), and
cross-language equal-work measurement
requiring no instrumentation of the production C++ sources. The resulting bound is
\emph{layered}: enforced by construction and property-tested on the work axis, parametric in
the caller's contract ($P$-affine,
two tiers), and empirical only in the time-per-operation factor. On every adversarial fixture
the Rust port meets or beats the C++ engine's observed maximum while eliminating its per-point
heap-allocation hazard, and the per-input equal-work certification proved sharp enough to
find, and fix, port-fidelity gaps that conventional differential testing missed.

\paragraph{Future work}
On the bare-metal AArch64 evaluation target both legs are now demonstrated: the counters
transfer exactly and the Profile-C timing series shows sub-percent spread
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-raspi4}); what remains there is the multi-core interference analog
(kernel tasks as co-runners) and a certified per-operation cost bound that would promote
$\text{work} \times \text{unit cost}$ to an analytical WCET (Sec.~\ref{sec:threats}).
Since the serial engine is not schedulable at 10\,Hz on that target
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-raspi4}), the production ECU should be audited with the same on-target
$P$-sweep protocol; on the host, parallelizing the derivative passes (the port's
order-preserving, numerically identical rayon backend) recovers a further
${\approx}\parRustSpeedupFour\times$ at four cores and brings the deployment-tier witness
within the \SI{100}{ms} budget at two (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-parallel}).
The full POT/MLE EVT machinery was run and rejected by its own diagnostics
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-evt}); reviving extrapolation needs an independence-preserving
measurement protocol, not a better fit. Finally, the counter-guided transfer methodology
should apply to other data-dependent Autoware kernels.
