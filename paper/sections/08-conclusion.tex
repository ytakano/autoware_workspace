\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This case study shows how a high-fidelity, per-input-certified port can turn WCET analysis of a
data-dependent industrial kernel into
a tractable pipeline: a structural work bound enforced and
property-tested in the port, counter-extremal inputs found by a deterministic
counter-guided search with an exact saturation certificate for the dominant term, and
cross-language common-work measurement
requiring no instrumentation of the production C++ sources. The resulting evidence is
\emph{layered}: enforced by construction and property-tested on the work axis, parametric in
the caller's contract through a $P$-affine, two-tier model, and empirical only in the
time-per-operation factor. On the shared adversarial fixtures, the Rust port meets or beats
the C++ engine's observed maximum while eliminating its per-point heap-allocation hazard
within the reserved envelope. The per-input trace exposed \realBaseOnly{} base-only and
\realIterationDivergent{} iteration-divergent real frames that an iteration-only reading
would have missed.

Rust is not a prerequisite for the work bounds or real-time hardening reported here; an
equivalent C++ redesign is possible. Its role in this case study is to discharge the safe
core's memory-safety and data-race-freedom obligations through the language, so that
project-specific validation can concentrate on equivalence, numerical behavior, bounded
work, and timing. That reduction stops at the FFI, unsafe code, and trusted-dependency
boundaries identified in Sec.~\ref{sec:threats}.

\paragraph{Operational validation requirements}
The evidence reported here is component-level and does not establish production readiness.
Before deployment, the port must be evaluated on the production ECU with representative
maps, scans, initial guesses, and co-resident Autoware workloads. That campaign must
characterize governor and boost behavior, multi-core scheduling and interference, and
end-to-end compliance with the 10\,Hz deadline. It must also assess closed-loop localization
behavior, including quality-gate decisions and filter innovations, on the ill-conditioned
segments where the implementations can diverge. The serial engine is not schedulable at
10\,Hz on the bare-metal AArch64 target, so the production ECU evaluation must repeat the
on-target $P$-sweep protocol. The host rayon result in Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-parallel} is only
throughput and deadline-feasibility evidence; it does not establish multi-core WCET. An
analytical WCET claim additionally requires a certified per-operation cost bound under the
deployment hardware and interference model. Likewise, the rejected POT/MLE diagnostics in
Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-evt} mean that probabilistic extrapolation requires an
independence-preserving measurement protocol rather than a different tail fit.
