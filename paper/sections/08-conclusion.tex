\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We showed that a bit-exact port turns WCET analysis of a data-dependent industrial kernel into
a tractable, largely automated pipeline: a structural work bound enforced and machine-checked
in the port, worst inputs found by a deterministic counter-guided search (with an exact
saturation certificate for the dominant term), and cross-language equal-work measurement
requiring no instrumentation of the production C++ sources. The resulting bound is
\emph{layered}: enforced by construction and machine-checked on the work axis, parametric in
the caller's contract ($P$-affine,
two tiers), and empirical only in the time-per-operation factor. On every adversarial fixture
the Rust port meets or beats the C++ engine's observed maximum while eliminating its per-point
heap-allocation hazard --- and the per-input equal-work certification proved sharp enough to
find, and fix, port-fidelity gaps that conventional differential testing missed.

\paragraph{Future work}
The counter transfer to the bare-metal AArch64 deployment target is demonstrated
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-raspi4}); what remains there is the controlled \emph{timing} protocol
(repeats, warm/cold series, an interference co-runner) that would promote the work bound
toward an analytical WCET (Sec.~\ref{sec:threats}). Also: statistically stronger EVT
(peaks-over-threshold, maximum-likelihood fits, confidence intervals); applying the
counter-guided transfer methodology to other data-dependent Autoware kernels.
