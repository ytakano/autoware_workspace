\begin{abstract}
The execution time of Normal Distributions Transform (\ndt) scan matching is strongly
input-dependent, complicating worst-case execution-time (WCET) analysis on the localization
critical path. We analyze the align kernel of the industrial Autoware matcher; map loading,
preprocessing, and ROS scheduling are outside the scope. The Rust port reduces the
low-level verification surface of the safe core and supports \texttt{no\_std}, but bounded
execution is not Rust-specific and the FFI, unsafe code, and dependencies remain trusted.
Our three-layer method establishes a conditional structural work bound, searches Rust
instrumentation counters for stress inputs, and measures frozen witnesses. It separates
algorithmic work from platform-specific time per unit work and does not claim a certified
hard time bound.
The search saturates the Rust envelope's analytic maximum for the common
kernel-evaluation term and increases the Rust implementation's kd-node count. C++ timing
remains an observation over the transferred fixture set, not a search for a C++ traversal
extremum. Per-input traces provide evidence that Rust and C++ perform the same recorded work
shape on trace-conformant inputs.
We call this relation work-trace conformance; leaf payloads, implementation-specific
traversal, and latency remain distinct. The input-domain analysis separates the
bounded-neighbor engine contract from the production preprocessing contract, while the
experiments separately identify engine-configuration and shipped-configuration stress.
In a cropped-map, degraded-prior stress replay drawn from a 57-minute drive, only
\realOnMap{} frames perform on-map matching; \realShapeOnMap{} satisfy both base agreement
and work-trace conformance, \realBaseOnly{} satisfy base agreement only, and
\realBaseDivergent{} fall outside the base criterion. Of the last group,
\realIterationDivergent{} diverge in iteration count. Across the primary and counter-Pareto
frontier frozen-fixture campaigns, the engine-specific observed maxima are
\SI{\combinedFixtureMaxRust}{ms} for Rust and \SI{\combinedFixtureMaxCpp}{ms} for C++.
The Rust port also removes approximately \allocPerPtPass{} heap allocations per point per
derivative pass within its reserved envelope.
\end{abstract}
