\begin{abstract}
The execution time of Normal Distributions Transform (\ndt) scan matching is strongly
input-dependent, complicating worst-case execution-time (WCET) analysis on the localization
critical path. We analyze the align kernel of the industrial Autoware matcher; map loading,
preprocessing, and ROS scheduling are outside scope. The Rust port reduces the
low-level verification surface of the safe core and supports \texttt{no\_std}, but bounded
execution is not Rust-specific and the FFI, unsafe code, and dependencies remain trusted.
Our three-layer method establishes a structural work bound, searches deterministic cost
counters for extremal inputs, and measures frozen witnesses. It separates algorithmic work
from platform-specific time per unit work and does not claim a certified hard time bound.
The search saturates the analytic maximum number of kernel evaluations and increases the
remaining kd-traversal term. Per-input traces provide evidence that Rust and C++ perform the same
recorded common-work
structure on work-trace-conformant inputs, while implementation-specific traversal and
latency remain distinct. A two-tier model
separates the bounded-neighbor engine domain from preprocessing-reachable inputs; their
measured witnesses differ by $\legalWorstRatioRust\times$--$\legalWorstRatioCpp\times$.
In a cropped-map, degraded-prior stress replay drawn from a 57-minute drive, only
\realOnMap{} frames perform on-map matching; \realTraceOnMap{} are work-trace-conformant,
\realBaseOnly{} satisfy only the base-agreement criterion, and \realIterationDivergent{}
diverge in
iteration count. On frozen synthetic fixtures, Rust
meets or improves the C++ observed maximum and removes approximately \allocPerPtPass{}
per-point heap allocations per derivative pass within its reserved envelope.
\end{abstract}
